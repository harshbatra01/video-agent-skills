# Sports music edits for song promotion

What they are for: music promo pages, where the client's song is the audio and must feel like the star; the athlete
and the effects are the vehicle (13 to 14 Oct 2026). Effects, flashes, type, beat sync and the structure library:
`motion-design`. Footage and song rights: `research-and-sourcing`.

## 1. The song is the product
- The song is audible from frame 0. Run its intro under any quote, or open on a wordless stare; long spoken cold opens
  bury it.
- Put the song in the post title, credit it in the caption, and link the exact audio version in the description.
- End on the song: a loop, or a live (moving) song card naming the track. Our first edit ended on a static streaming-app
  card for its last 3.2 s, one of the gaps the research found against the top edits.
- Title grammar that holds the biggest numbers: "Player - SONG (Artist) - Skills & Goals" (181.8M views), "SONG - ARTIST
  (version) x SUBJECT" (12.6M), "[subject] x [artist]" (4.3M).
- Run honest edit pages that credit the song: label-run fake fan accounts draw "industry plant" backlash (Dazed, 7 Oct
  2026).
- An athlete really using the song is the strongest seed: a star walking out to a song coincided with it reaching Shazam
  #2 worldwide, and the pilot's song was picked because the player and a top club had both used it. Check whether the
  subject has used the song before choosing the pairing.
- Ask clients for edit-ready versions of the song: slowed + reverb, sped up, instrumental, a no-beat-switch version, and
  a clean 20 to 30 s cut with a clear drop. Why: edits ride modified versions (38% of TikTok songs were speed or pitch
  modified in 2023, per Pex) and edit-audio compilation pages reach 8M to 13M; labels can ship these on release day.

## 2. Benchmarks (YouTube, 75 edits with 150K+ views)
- Length: median 39 s (middle half 27 to 56 s).
- Like rate: median 2.8%; the top 10% above 4.2%.
- Comments: a median 0.49 per 1,000 views.

## 3. Where the effort pays
- Hooks beat craft for reach: the most inventive motion-graphic edits often have under 3K views while hook-led Shorts
  get 2M to 28M. Invest in the first second.
- The letterboxed band layout (the picture in a 1080x800 band with black bars, type in the bars) is fine on Reels: top
  Instagram edits post widescreen inside the vertical feed (1.5M to 2.4M plays). The editor asked for it ("its not
  NECESSARY that we HAVE to use full frame"), overturning research that said use the whole vertical frame.
- Openings: 41% of 69 YouTube edits have their most-replayed moment in the first 10%. Frame 1 is already moving on the
  downbeat, one beat before the move (starting at the start of a play is "the most common amateur tell"); a famous face
  in frame 1; a quiet open beats a shouted one; the song's hook is the first visual hit.
- The payoff is a dense 2 to 10 s zone at 55% to 70% of the runtime, not one hit: replay maps show two peaks (the first
  second and 55% to 70%), and only 3 of 69 edits peaked at the end. (The editor's own first edit dropped at 20%.)
- Loops drive rewatches: a 13 s duo loop did 8.5M in under 3 months. Make a 13 s loop version of every edit, where the
  end plays toward the opening frame (research rule, untested).
- Pick the song window from data: start one beat before the drop, include the song's most-replayed stretch, end on a
  section's last downbeat plus one held bar (an ending that cut off early drew "end cuts off sooner than it should").
- The bar: "we are the best in the world... absolutely crazy editors go so hard"; good is not enough ("good wont cut we
  need GREAT"). The editor's own first edit was the starting point, not the bar ("it nEEDS TO BE MUCH BETTER"); the bar
  is the consensus top of the inspiration bank.

## 4. Bases to try next (with the evidence)
- A 13 s duo loop (8.5M).
- Idol x famous fan: a second famous face in the first second.
- A velocity edit: every clip at 30% to 40% speed.
- An end payoff: the strongest image on the loudest hit in the last 2 s (a cheap A/B, since few edits do it).
- A manager or legend persona on UK rap (4.3M).
- Then vs now: B&W past, colour released on the drop.
- A ranked countdown (real data only).
- A meme template: a 1 s copyable action on the hook.
- A two-song switch; a skit or question hook; a world-swap metaphor.
- The structures already built and measured (twelve bases, chosen per song, subject and footage): `motion-design`.

## 5. The SOP versions for any niche
Sports Edits and Military Edits from the music-page SOPs: the client song replaces the synced reference song so its drop
lands on the edit's drop; most templates are high BPM (rap, drill), a few low BPM ones fit any song; fade the last 1 s;
peak about -3 dB; export 1080x1920 or 1920x1080 per template; for military footage, match the song to its intense,
cinematic energy first (`music-news-pages.md` section 3.3).

## 6. Facts and footage
- Every number on screen (a birth date, a record, a draft pick) is double-checked before posting; a bare number means
  nothing, so each one gets a caption that says what it is.
- Source quality is part of the look: heavy effects on low-resolution footage look cheap, and the top packs advertise 4K
  at 60 to 120 fps. Ask for 4K packs when downloads are blocked.
- Rights: scene packs carry only "fair use" boilerplate; named-star match footage has no clean free source; labels can
  claim edits that use major-label songs on YouTube (`research-and-sourcing`).

## 7. The model already runs at scale elsewhere
An artist's album was released as one anime-edit mix credited to dozens of editors, with per-track campaigns paying
editors per 1,000 views (rates in the research file, not in this kit). Useful evidence for the reviewer's pitch.
