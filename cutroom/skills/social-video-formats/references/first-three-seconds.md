# The first three seconds: the click-through test

In a feed nobody clicks a thumbnail first. The first seconds ARE the click: a viewer who stays past them is the feed's
version of a click-through. How to find and cut the hook moment (replay heatmaps, transcripts, beats): `editing-playbook`.

## 0. What each platform measures at the start
- Instagram Reels have no CTR. The nearest number is the skip rate: the share of views that leave inside the first 3 s
  (in Insights since Aug 2025). 100% minus the skip rate is the closest thing to a CTR. 2026 averages sit at 60.5% to
  65.5% (`measuring-performance.md`).
- TikTok's creative guidance: state the proposition by 3 s, land the hook by 6 s.
- YouTube long form: the "Intro" figure is the share still watching at 30 s. YouTube Shorts research: the hook inside
  2.5 s.
- Facebook long videos (one page's data): half the viewers had left by about 21 s.
- Frame 0 is also the cover: it becomes the grid thumbnail and the still people see before anything moves.

## 1. Rules for every platform
- **Frame 0 carries the hook.** The headline is fully on screen from frame 0 (no fade-in, no logo intro), a face is big,
  and the name and the conflict come first. Why: a face early is the one first-frame tip with some support, and frame 0
  doubles as the cover.
- **Sound starts on the hook,** not on an intro. Audio is on for over 80% of Reels plays (Meta advice, secondhand), so
  the first sound is part of the hook; text still carries it for muted viewers.
- **Something real moves in the first second**: the footage, a live data line, a punch-in. Not a slide-in or a counting
  number (both ad tells on native pages).
- **Every number on frame 0 is final and true**, never mid-count. Motion renders start 0.5 s in, so frame 0 is never an
  empty frame.
- **No logo or brand name on frame 0** of a native post (an ad tell), and never open a paid ad on the logo either.
- **The 2-second test**: freeze frame 0 and frame 60 (2 s at 30 fps). A stranger must be able to name the subject, the
  number and why it matters. If not, rewrite the opening before building the rest.
- **Few words, front-loaded**: the words that must land in the first second, 6 or fewer, on a plate in the middle zone;
  full headlines 6 to 10 words with the key words first. On-screen text runs at about 15 characters a second (Netflix
  caps reading at 20), never under 1.5 s. For explainers: hold text max(1.0, 0.4 + 0.33 x words) s, headline at most 7
  words, 42 characters a line, 2 lines.
- **Inside the safe zone**: headline and face between about 15% and 65% of the height on 9:16
  (`platforms-and-policies.md` section 3).
- **A real clip beats a spoken hook**: one page posted the same story two minutes apart; the real-clip cold open got 7.6K
  views, the spoken hook 2.3K.
- **A tease must pay off fast**: a curiosity gap answered after second 6 probably raises the skip rate (inference).

## 2. Hooks that work, by format
| Format | What works | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Music and rap news | conflict with a clock ("reply drops Thursday 7pm"); "X just did Y" with a date; a surprising number; a curiosity gap paid off by second 6; quote first, then the name | no study ranks hooks for music news; these recur on the UK pages that win; two beef posts on a fast UK page reached 733K and 825K views on X |
| Faceless brand pages | a situation, a shock or a POV, never the product; 4 to 12 words, no greeting, no product jargon ("odds"), no venue name | "When your parlay keep losing by 1": 488K views on 4,010 followers; "POV: [a president] tweeted during your trade": 647K on 622; odds-titled countdowns ran at 0.22x their page's followers vs 10x to 105x for plain ones |
| Story reels (narrated mini-docs) | about 12 words with a famous name or a number, finished by about 3.5 s | number hooks 49.5K median vs plain claims 5.7K on one studied page (`reel-scripts`) |
| Founder and expert reels | "[someone] just [did X]" or a contradiction; a name or number inside 3 s; line two an open loop ("Let me explain") | 21 measured reels (`reel-scripts`) |
| Documentary cutdowns (long cuts) | 4 single-line beats in 7 to 9 s: premise, threat, action, twist closer, cut to white; one continuous 3 to 4 s moment for a single incident | the editor trimmed every hook over 12 s, loved 10 to 11 s, then asked for "a bit shorter" |
| Sports music edits | frame 1 already moving on the downbeat, one beat before the move; a famous face in frame 1; a quiet open beats a shouted one; the song audible from frame 0; the song's hook is the first visual hit | 41% of 69 YouTube edits have their most-replayed moment in the first 10%; starting at the start of a play is "the most common amateur tell" |
| Paid supplement ads | open mid-action on the strongest image with a stakes line; the key message inside 3 s; the product in use by 3 to 5 s with real sounds; specific direct address ("if you signed up for a marathon this year") | research plus the reviewer's script notes (`presenter-ads.md`) |
| YouTube review episodes | a contrast hook in the first 5 s ("$500 vs $30": price tells you nothing); the thesis inside 10 s | research, 4 Oct |
| News reels with a presenter | opening on a split for the first 1.5 to 3 s (proof on top, presenter below) is worth borrowing | a reference-set measured reference look |

- Urgency words (JUST DROPPED, OUT NOW, DROPS TONIGHT AT 7PM) only on time-sensitive stories; on everything else they
  read as noise.
- Never injury, death or the final outcome in a hook: it spoils the payoff and, on Facebook, risks a warning screen.

## 3. Music news reels, second by second (7 to 15 s)
| Time | Do |
|---|---|
| 0.0 s | headline fully on screen, no fade; a face big; sound starts on the hook; no logo intro |
| 0 to 3 s | something moves in the first second; the headline stays (the skip window) |
| 3 to 7 s | the fact (who, what, when) plus our added number, time or date |
| 7 to 12 s | proof: the video's peak or an interview line; captions only on speech |
| last 2 s | a clock or an open question (never "comment X"); end on a frame like frame 0 so it loops; no outro card |

## 4. Carousels: slide 1 and slide 2
- Slide 1 works like frame 0: a claim or a number plus a face, readable on the 3:4 grid crop.
- Slide 2 is a second cover: Instagram often re-serves a carousel someone scrolled past starting on slide 2 (Mosseri,
  17 Oct 2024). Give it its own hook and the key number in different words; never "continued"; it must make sense
  without slide 1.

## 5. Endings that loop
- Music news reels: the last 2 s end on a frame like frame 0, with no outro card.
- Sports edits: a 13 s loop version where the end plays toward the opening frame (research rule, untested; a 13 s duo
  loop did 8.5M views).
- A Short that ends on its verdict badge loops back into the hook.
- No official word on how replays count: read the retention chart after 24 h.

## 6. Testing hooks
- Instagram Trial Reels (10 Dec 2024): a reel goes to non-followers first and stays off the grid; numbers after about
  24 h. A/B two openings of the day's best story, changing only the first 3 s. Check for the toggle on day 1 (a
  1,000-follower minimum is reported, UNVERIFIED). At 0 followers every reel reaches only non-followers anyway.
- Without the toggle, run alternate openings on alternate days.
- Facebook: judge hook A/B tests by the 3-second view rate (proposed, untested).
- After 24 h read the retention chart: a cliff in 0 to 3 s means rewrite frame 0; a cliff mid-reel means cut there.
