# Platforms and policies (as of Oct 2026)

Platform rules change often. Every rule below carries the date it was read. Re-check the platform's own page before
relying on a rule for a client decision, then update this file with the new date. "(UNVERIFIED)" marks claims seen only
in secondary reports or vendor posts. Legal and claims checks (advertising codes, consumer law, gambling and finance law)
belong to `research-and-sourcing`; this file says what each platform rule changes about what we make.

## 0. Ask at intake, before anything is built
- Where will it be posted, on which account type (Creator, personal or Business), organic or paid? Why: the platform and
  the account type decide which music and footage are acceptable, the length, the safe zones and the disclosure.
- Confirm the format: aspect ratio, resolution, frame rate. A whole storyboard was once built in 9:16 because "reel"
  sounded vertical; the client wanted 1920x1080 ("you should have asked me that"). The example sequence and the
  references are the format signal; ask when there are none.
- Read the client's own rules file first (banned words, closing line, what must never be covered, AI voice allowed or
  not).

## 2. Platform by platform

### 2.1 Instagram (Reels, carousels, Stories)
- **No follower gate.** A new reel goes to a small test audience and widens on watch time, likes per reach and sends per
  reach (Mosseri, 22 Jan 2025). Likes count a little more for followers, sends a little more for non-followers; comments
  and saves were not named. What it means: every post is judged alone from post one, so reels are the growth engine and
  cards support them; aim for posts people send to a friend.
- **Skip rate** (in Insights since Aug 2025, replacing view rate) = the share of views that leave inside the first 3 s.
  There is no organic CTR; the first 3 s are the click (`first-three-seconds.md`, `measuring-performance.md`).
- **Reach and engagement by format** (vendor data): at 1K to 5K followers reach rate is Reels 9.78%, carousels 8.80%,
  images 7.00%; engagement (Q2 2026) carousels 0.50%, Reels 0.48%, images 0.33%. Another source: carousels 6.9% of reach
  vs Reels 3.3%, and about 9x the saves of single images (Metricool, 24.4M posts, 16 Jun 2026), while Reels still reach
  about 1.36x more than carousels (Buffer, data to Oct 2024). The levels disagree; both agree carousels lead engagement
  and saves and Reels lead reach.
- **Length is a test, not a rule.** Brand-page reach was 5.2% under 30 s vs 5.6% at 30 to 60 s, and typical views 4,700
  vs 10,374 at 45 to 60 s (Socialinsider, Jun and Jul 2026). Average reel watch time is about 8.5 s (2026), up from 4 s.
- **Originality rule (30 Apr 2026).** Accounts that mostly post unoriginal reels, photos or carousels leave
  recommendations; followers still see them. "Mostly" is not defined and it is judged per account. Original = you made
  it, or you materially edited it (on-image text that adds context, graphics that add new information, remix).
  Not original: borders, watermarks, subtitles, credit lines, speed changes, a screenshot of someone's post with their
  username. Recovery: most posts original over a rolling 30 days. Check Settings > Account > Account Status for
  standing and appeals. What it means: one added fact on every post (`content-bar.md` section 3).
- **Engagement bait.** Instagram will not recommend content that "explicitly asks for engagement through shares,
  comments, tags, or other actions", such as a set word, number or emoji (5 Jun 2024). Open questions are fine.
- **Hashtags** are capped at 5 (Dec 2025); Mosseri says they add context, not reach. Use 0 to 2.
- **Trial Reels** (10 Dec 2024): a reel goes to non-followers first, hidden from the grid and followers; numbers after
  about 24 h; optional auto-share within 72 h. Eligibility is unclear (a 1,000-follower minimum is reported,
  UNVERIFIED): check for the toggle on day 1. Use it to A/B the first 3 s.
- **Slide 2 is a second cover**: Instagram often re-serves a carousel someone scrolled past, starting on slide 2
  (Mosseri, 17 Oct 2024). Carousels with music can also appear in the Reels tab.
- **Grid crop**: the profile grid has shown 3:4 since Jan 2025, so a 1080x1920 cover loses 240 px top and bottom.
- **Screenshots of other accounts** are demoted (a reading of the originality rule): draw our own tweet cards, never
  screenshot other pages.
- **Suggestive posts**: Instagram limits recommendations of thirst-trap content. It does not recommend "people
  fighting".
- **Professional account type**: use a Creator account (or personal), never Business, on music pages. Why: Business
  accounts lose the full music library.
- **Platform UI in ads**: Meta needs permission to show Instagram's interface in an ad: design your own question or
  comment card instead.

### 2.2 TikTok
- **For You drives about 7 in 10 views** (Metricool, 12 May 2026).
- **2026H2 Community Guidelines** (released 25 Aug 2026, in force 24 Sep 2026; seen through secondary reports): content
  is ineligible for For You "if it includes unoriginal or reused material without anything new". The 2025H2 text already
  made reused content without creative edits ineligible. What it means: the same added-fact rule as Instagram, and plain
  clip reposts get nothing.
- **Watermarks**: strip third-party watermarks (other apps, CapCut trial marks): TikTok drops such posts from For You.
- **Networks of accounts**: TikTok bans "networks of similar accounts distributing high volume commercial content".
  Vendors report flags from identical file hashes, caption structures and posting schedules across related accounts
  (UNVERIFIED). What it means: pages in one network differ in niche, look and posting times, never share a file.
- **Video vs photo mode**: video got 5.6x the views and 7.8x the interactions of image or carousel posts (Metricool,
  Jan to Feb 2026, 2.31M posts); median engagement video 3.39% vs photo posts 1.92% (Buffer, 19 Mar 2026). Run both:
  carousels for volume and saves, video for reach.
- **Hit or nothing**: the median TikTok post gets about 500 views at any cadence (Buffer, 11.4M posts).
- **Creator pay**: TikTok pays creators only for original videos over 1 minute.
- **Hook timing**: TikTok's creative guidance says state the proposition by 3 s and land the hook by 6 s.
- **Graphic content**: TikTok can keep graphic police clashes off For You.
- **Music by account type**: Business accounts see only the Commercial Music Library; the general library is licensed for
  personal, non-commercial use; many trending tracks are not cleared for business; baking a commercial song into
  "original sound" does not license it. Ask the client which account type the pages use before choosing music.
- **Ads**: TikTok's guidance recommends 21 to 34 s for ads; TopView (the full-screen ad shown when the app opens) bans
  explosions, screams, phone rings and alarms in the first 3 s (so avoid notification-like blips in any hook). Ads need
  a Business Account, music added in TikTok's editor and the licence box ticked; a known bug mutes audio after product
  links.
- **Branded content** (policy published 4 Aug 2026, in force 31 Aug 2026): switch on the commercial content disclosure
  toggle on any paid or per-view post; the post is labelled Branded Content. Undisclosed commercial content can lose For
  You (there are 24 h to answer a notice) and repeated failure can bring posting limits. Gambling and investment-type
  finance are restricted to brands with TikTok's permission, a registered business account and TikTok One (brands may
  only work with creators through TikTok One). TikTok's prediction-market ad policy (Aug 2026) needs trading or
  event-contract wording.
- **Reading TikTok from a blocked country**: research routes are in `research-and-sourcing`.
- UNVERIFIED gaps: TikTok's grid crop, and any music effect on photo mode (no test data found).

### 2.3 YouTube (Shorts and long form)
- **AI personas** (monetisation policy, "AI Personas Related to Sensitive Topics", checked on the policy page 4 Oct 2026):
  an AI persona that presents itself as a human health expert "will not be allowed to monetize"; YouTube's example is
  "An AI 'doctor' providing medical diagnoses". The same page treats AI personas presenting as finance experts as a
  problem (its example: an AI host giving investment tips). What it means: no credential claims for an AI presenter.
- **Altered or synthetic content box**: tick "Altered content: Yes" on every video and Short with a photoreal AI person.
  Since 27 May 2026 undisclosed photoreal AI gets labelled automatically; repeated non-disclosure risks Partner Program
  suspension; health videos get a more prominent label. An in-voice disclosure also works ("And no, I didn't film this
  one either.").
- **Templated and reused content**: a channel can lose monetisation for content that "looks like it's made with a
  template" or feels repetitive over several videos. Listed examples: image slideshows or scrolling text with minimal or
  no narrative, commentary or educational value; AI content from generic templates that looks mass produced; readings of
  text from websites or news feeds; reused content with no real changes, even with permission. Allowed: the same intro
  and outro if the bulk differs; AI for scripts or a unique background visual; tools and templates when the result shows
  creative vision. (A July 2026 clarification adding AI personas and manipulative clips is UNVERIFIED.)
- **Medical misinformation**: nothing that contradicts health authorities, never link a device to cancer, citations on
  screen.
- **Gambling**: since 19 Mar 2025 any link, URL, logo or spoken mention that sends viewers to a gambling site not
  certified by Google is not allowed; promising guaranteed returns can get a video removed; casino-type content is
  age-restricted. No YouTube policy names prediction markets. What it means: for a gambling-adjacent brand, do not show,
  say or link the brand on YouTube until the client confirms how Google classes it.
- **Paid promotion**: the tickbox shows viewers a disclosure for 10 s at the start; UK guidance also wants "Ad" in the
  title or thumbnail before the click.
- **Chapters**: the first at 00:00, at least 3, each 10 s or longer.
- **End screens**: the last 5 to 20 s, at most 4 elements. Leave clear space and let the editor add the elements in
  YouTube Studio; zones on 1920x1080 are in premiere-ae-editing `references/motion-design.md`.
- **Mid-roll**: put the ad marker on a chapter boundary so it lands on a natural break.
- **Shorts** (research, 4 Oct 2026): hook inside 2.5 s, a change every 3 to 8 s, captions of 1 to 6 words, end on the
  finished state. (UNVERIFIED research claim: from 1 Feb 2027 Shorts earn ad revenue only for channels with 10M Shorts
  views in the last 90 days. Verify before planning revenue on Shorts.)
- **Long-form retention** (vendor samples, starting points): thesis inside 10 s; intros 24 to 72 s; "Intro" = the share
  still watching at 30 s; 7 to 13 visual changes a minute; never 60 s static; none of 22 measured top videos had an
  outro.
- **Long-form clips (from reference clips work)**: write the title and thumbnail promise before choosing in and out
  points; one distinct promise per clip (never the episode title); borrow title grammar from the guest's and host's best
  uploads; cut the episode trailer, sponsor reads and outro; open on a 10 to 15 s cold open from the clip's own payoff;
  thumbnail = a face + on-screen proof + 2 to 4 words; flag numbers a title cannot back. Mine the comments for demand.
  (Cutting: `editing-playbook`; trailers and cold opens: `podcast-trailers`.)
- **Swears and listed words**: meme compilations for YouTube blur every word against YouTube's guidelines, mild ones and
  usernames included (`meme-compilation-edit`). News reels star out swears in subtitles.
- **Music claims**: labels can claim edits that use major-label songs on YouTube (`research-and-sourcing`).
- The YouTube Trending page is gone (21 Jul 2025); trend sources are in `research-and-sourcing`.
- Not yet researched: the music and sound rules for Shorts. Check before relying on a song in a Short.

### 2.4 Facebook
- **Every video is a Reel** (since Jun 2025) with no length limit; landscape plays inline in the feed and letterboxed full
  screen.
- **Ranking** predicts watch time, finishing, skips and full-screen taps (researched 11 Oct 2026).
- **Originality (Mar 2026)**: reused footage counts only when the edit adds information, analysis or a better
  storyline; captions, borders or speed changes alone do not count; repeat offenders lose recommendations and
  monetisation. What it means: re-cut, never re-upload.
- **Graphic-violence warning screen**: death, serious injury or violence against restrained people put a warning screen
  on the post, and a warning screen makes it non-recommendable. Where recommendations drive most of a page's watch
  time, one screen costs most of a post's views.
- **Monetisation** is invite-only; views under 5 s and videos under 10 s do not count.
- **Engagement bait**: Facebook has its own engagement-bait rules (Meta Transparency Center); the same
  questions-not-orders approach applies. Penalty: less reach, not removal.
- **Gambling and crypto branded content** need Meta's written pre-authorisation (Instagram too); binary options and CFD
  trading are banned from branded content; gambling content is shown to adults only; guaranteed-win and get-rich-quick
  offers are removed.
- **Paid partnership tool** plus clear ad text on any paid post (Instagram and Facebook).
- **Muted viewing**: Meta says feed ads should still work muted, but audio is on for over 80% of Reels plays (secondhand).
- **Data access**: the built-in browser cannot open facebook.com; ask the page owner for Meta Business Suite exports
  (`measuring-performance.md` section 9).

### 2.5 X
- **Where news breaks**: the fast UK beef pages post on X first (one posts about 6 times a day, about 1.5 h after the
  artist's own upload, and mirrors to TikTok within a minute); two of its beef posts reached 733K and 825K views on X.
  We read X for detection; we confirm elsewhere (`research-and-sourcing`).
- **X size does not carry over**: a page with about 6.9M followers on X had about 10.6K on TikTok. TikTok's leaders are
  video-native brands.
- **Receipt-style posts** (a screen of a position or a win) go on X and Instagram only, never TikTok, where they read as
  gambling cues.
- **Paid partnerships**: gambling banned since Feb 2026 (reported 18 to 20 Feb 2026; X ads are governed separately);
  a Paid Partnership label exists since 1 to 2 Mar 2026 and can be added after posting.
- Reading X without a login uses an unofficial mirror that may break X's terms (`research-and-sourcing`).

## 3. Safe zones on 1080x1920
| Context | Keep key text and faces | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Organic Reels and TikTok (our pages) | between about 15% and 65% of the height (y ~290 to ~1250); inside x 120 to 918; nothing in the bottom 20% or the right 160 px | the 3:4 grid crop takes 240 px top and bottom; app buttons cover the right and the bottom |
| Covers and frame 0 | inside the central 1080x1350 band | the grid shows 3:4 |
| Text size | at most 2 sizes, nothing under 40 px (nothing under ~26 px ever) | phone readability |
| Paid vertical ads (research) | clear of the top ~14% and bottom ~35% (about 270 and 670 px); the pixel version also quoted, about 220 px top and 420 px bottom, is a smaller margin: treat 220/420 as the hard limit and 14%/35% as the target | ad UI and the CTA button cover the bottom |
| 4:5 feed crops | a top-left title near the top is cut; move it to about 250 px from the top | feed crop and the Reels or Stories header |
| Letterboxed music edits | type between 10% and 76% of the height | the band layout's own safe lines |
- Known exception: approved paid ads put dynamic captions low (kicker baseline y 1370, main caption y 1590 over a bottom
  gradient from y 980). They passed review but were never checked in an ad preview: check before launch (tentative).
- Never put text over a face or a mouth.

## 5. Disclosure on the post
- Anything paid is an ad, including pay per view: a plain "Ad" in the first line of the caption or on the first frame
  AND the platform's toggle (TikTok content disclosure, Instagram and Facebook Paid partnership, YouTube paid promotion,
  X Paid Partnership). UK and US regulators both say the platform label alone may not be enough. The legal detail
  (accepted wording, carousels, finance overlays): `research-and-sourcing`.
- AI content: label it where the platform expects it (YouTube's box) and where the audience would assume a real person.
- Voice clones only with the person's written consent, their sign-off on outputs, and AI labels (`reel-scripts`).

## 6. Delivery specs common to all platforms
- Audio: no platform publishes an upload loudness target. Deliver about -14 LUFS integrated (-14 to -16 for ads), true
  peak at or under -1 dBTP, 48 kHz. Renders that peak above -1 dBTP get a normalised version (one round of ad renders
  hit +0.1 dBTP). Mechanics: `premiere-ae-editing`.
- Captions: ask per job whether they are burned in. The paid ads were exported with the caption track burned in; some
  deliveries want them separate. On music posts, captions are for speech only, never lyrics.
- Shorts featuring a child: check the platform's rules on minors and the source's own blurring before posting.
