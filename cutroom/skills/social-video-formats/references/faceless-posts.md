# Faceless brand pages: real moments, one native caption, the product as the stakes

A faceless page posts for a brand with no creator on camera: memes, borrowed moments, the product's real screen,
carousels. The worked case was a trading and prediction-market brand (13 to 14 Oct 2026), so the examples use chances
and prices. The rules carry to any brand whose product produces a number (a price, a chance, a score, a stat): read
"the market" as "the product's own number". Research method (frames, not thumbnails): `research-and-sourcing`. Building
the posts: `production-automation` (the post builder) and `editing-playbook`.

## 1. What failed first, and what it taught
| Round | What was built | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 18 designed data cards in one family, the number as the product | "these are all SAME types of posts bro" |
| 2 | 40 motion-graphic videos (forecasts, races, countdowns, quizzes, dashboards, look skins, SFX packs, about 37 words on screen) | "the UI is bad", "not worth posting", "I can't watch them" |
| 3 | a plan page of researched formats, mocked in the TikTok white-box caption style | the style: "TOOO off beat"; the formats: "based on research do the best formats cook them up" |
| 4 | 9 posts in the house look (borrowed moments, the real app screen, one caption) | pending at 14 Oct |
- Diagnosis: data graphics with numbers on them have no hook and do not look native in a feed, and rounds 1 and 2 never
  looked at real posts frame by frame ("I don't think you researched enough").
- Rule: start from proven native formats with real footage and a simple caption, then fit the product into them. Do not
  patch weak posts to make them "watchable". Research real viral examples (frames, views against page size) and show a
  plan page with proof frames and open decisions before building a batch ("Nothing new gets built until you've
  decided").
- Borrowed footage is fine as long as no creator is on camera (editor decision): background clips, lifestyle footage,
  film and aura edits, other people's clips.

## 2. The number is the punchline or the stakes, never the product
- People share a moment that has a number attached; a price alone is not content. The evidence is in
  `content-bar.md` section 5 (a bookmaker's price posts at 4 to 13 likes vs its banter with a number as the punchline at
  2,911 to 23,133).
- One number per frame, attached to a face or a moment; tags in brackets: "(2%)". Absurd sizes travel: 0.1%, 2%, 9%, 92%.

## 3. Most posts carry no number
- Moments and memes build the audience; number posts spend it.
- Evidence (14 Oct): none of the biggest prediction market's 20 most-viewed sports TikToks mentions a price; its football
  page's biggest post (a star before and after, 41.8M views, 129x its followers) has no odds; a second prediction
  market's sports TikTok got 494K to 934K organic views a post on 7,516 followers. The app appears in about 1 post in
  6, as a short insert, and never as a number on the cover.

## 4. The daily mix per page
| Slot | Per day |
|---|---|
| Moments or memes (no number, or one number as the joke) | 4 |
| Punchline posts (the number lands the joke or the stakes) | 2 |
| A real-app post (the product's own screen as footage) | 1 |
| A carousel | 1 |
| The morning after a big event night (upsets, comebacks) | +2 |
- Volume: the benchmark pages post 6 to 12 times a day (one about 10.7). Run one account per topic: one per sport first
  (separate sports, football and basketball accounts is the leader's pattern), then crypto and macro.
- While testing: 1 to 3 posts a day per page; 5 posts per launch format over 2 weeks; judge a format after 30 to 50
  posts, not 5. Why: TikTok is hit or nothing (the median post gets about 500 views at any cadence; routine posts on a big
  brand's page got 1.1K to 1.9K while one framed as a joke got 421K).
- The morning-after posts come from the night's upsets: in one night favourites won 5 of 9; a 7% side won 4-3 away and a
  5% side drew 3-3. Flag upsets (the winner under 35% at kick-off) and comebacks (the winner under 25% at half time).
- Build a calendar of dated hooks before planning a week's batches: events with exact times and verified facts (a
  data release at 12:30 UTC, a rate decision at 18:00 UTC, an awards night), each with a source and a date or marked
  UNVERIFIED. Why: timely posts need exact dates, and "tonight" posts expire.

## 5. The shapes that win
### 5.1 "The line said X, then this happened"
- A longshot that landed or a favourite that fell, with the chance shown next to the moment that decided it. Why: the
  number makes the stakes after the fact. A favourite that simply wins has no story.
- Evidence: "took [team] NOT to win at 9%": 59,518 likes, 10.37M views; "$1M on [team] at 92%": 85,710 likes and 10,739
  quotes once the team failed to win; a league's "1% to 100%" comeback clip: 3.54M.
- Two numbers side by side make tension: the line vs the result, then vs now, before vs after, cost vs payout.
- Built example: "[TEAM A] WERE 79% TO BEAT [TEAM B]. [TEAM B] WON 4-3" over the winning goal.

### 5.2 Pick the number format by the metric you want
- Label memes earn shares ("[player] when his country needed him the most": 925,089 plays, 37,220 shares).
- Receipts (a real position screen) earn saves.
- A favourite-on-a-loser post earns quotes (10,739 on the $1M post).
- Price polls earn replies ("too high or too low?" drew 8.2% likes per view).

### 5.3 The format catalogue (14 Oct plan; proof = views, on followers where known)
- **(A) The number as the punchline**: "The money said 79%. Then this happened."; then vs now under a reaction face
  (6.14M on 1,640 followers); a "How did he know" smirk edit (only when honest, section 12); a reaction clip plus one
  line (488K on 4,010).
- **(B) The real app as footage**: a POV with the live screen and one emoji (647K on 622); a collapse filmed off a screen
  (510K on 2,850); a meltdown over the match page using a borrowed reaction clip.
- **(C) Borrowed footage plus one real number**: "I was 80% happy" (2.58M); "He says X, the money says Y" (1.5M on
  109K); the "hits different" lifestyle POV (4.36M); aura and football edits with a number beat.
- **(D) Carousels and text**: one star per slide (23.9M); "More likely than" (30,979 likes; built: "[a rate rise] (19%)
  is more likely than [a club] winning the league (4%)"); RIP cards; price polls.
- **The client's own formats, kept on the same rules**: a profit card over an aura edit (229K on 1,180); a hands-only
  how-to (2.81M on 12.8K); a big-trader story (2.01M on 65.8K).
- **Skip**: whale alerts (0 to 4 likes), daily wraps (under 300 views), higher-or-lower, "if you invested",
  broadcast clips with a % banner, weather skins as a hook, "Day N of turning $100 into...".
- Earlier research signals: topics and game mechanics beat "odds" content (#ballondor 34.45B views vs #footballodds
  1.6M); then-vs-now boards did 96x on a 3.93K page; stat face-offs 80x; plain countdowns 10x to 105x vs odds-titled
  ones at 0.22x.

### 5.4 Benchmark mechanics to copy
- The leading prediction market's football page on Instagram (325K followers, 1.21B views in 12 months, about 10.7 posts
  a day; a typical post gets 0.96x its follower count). Its hits are carousels: one star per slide, a giant %, a money
  line, the real app chart last. Keep the number identical on every slide (one of its posts showed 55%, 51% and 50.8% for
  the same thing: a mistake). 16 of its top 20 posts are carousels or photos.
- The second prediction market's sports page on TikTok: real fan or press footage, one caption, 8 to 17 s.
- Co-author Instagram posts with the brand's own account (a collab post) so its profile collects the views. Needs the
  client's OK. Many of the brands' top posts are co-authored with celebrities, creators or partner pages, so ratios on
  collab posts mean little.

## 6. The product goes in as a short insert, framed as the stakes
- Inserts last 1.5 to 8 s; 8 of 12 measured partner videos put the insert between about 20% and 77% of the runtime;
  clip pages put it at the end (84% to 100%).
- Brand on screen for at most 25% of the runtime unless the product moment is the story; one brand mention, at 50% to
  70%. A client creator guide's 8 to 10 s plug at 50% to 70% is longer than what the winners used.
- Show the product the way users see it: the real app screen (a screen recording, a crop, or the screen filmed on a phone)
  or one number in the text. Never a designed % card, gauge, bar, race or weather icon: the one automated "LIVE READ
  78% YES" card page found had 2 views. Every partner hit uses the real screen, which also shows the brand without an
  added logo and has no broadcast-rights problem (filming it: `production-automation`).
- Never put text over the app's chart, logo or buttons (a client rule), and captions match the app's own on-screen
  labels, not data polled behind it (a caption once said 52% while the app showed 70%).

## 7. Hooks
Situations, shocks or a POV, never the product: 4 to 12 words, no greeting, no product jargon, no venue name. Why:
people stop for a moment they recognise, not for a market. Evidence and the other formats' hooks:
`first-three-seconds.md` section 2.

## 8. Carousels and short clips: make both
- Carousels carry numbers; short clips carry moments; each platform rewards a different shape.
  - TikTok: video got 5.6x the views and 7.8x the interactions of image or carousel posts (Metricool, Jan to Feb 2026,
    2.31M posts); median engagement video 3.39% vs photo posts 1.92% (Buffer, 19 Mar 2026).
  - Instagram: carousels 6.9% engagement of reach vs Reels 3.3% (Buffer); about 9x the saves of single images.
- Specs: Instagram 1080x1350, 5 to 8 slides (10 for rankings); TikTok photo mode 5 slides. Slide 2 is a second cover
  with its own hook and the key number. The last slide can be the real app card.
- Long recaps fail: recaps of 77 to 161 s got only 21K to 95K.

## 9. Native, not designed: the house look and the ad tells
### 9.1 Why the designed look failed, in numbers
Our 22 videos measured against 49 no-face TikTok references (OCR on frames, 14 Oct):

| | Ours | Native |
|---|---|---|
| Readable words on screen at once | 10.5 | 4 |
| Tiny words (under 1.8% of frame height) | 27 | 0 |
| Text centre, % of the height from the top | 26% | 50% |
| Frames with any text | 100% | 88% |

### 9.2 Native text rules
- 4 to 9 readable words at a time; nothing tiny; at most 2 text sizes; nothing under 40 px.
- Text mid-screen, inside x 120 to 918 and y 270 to 1250 on 1080x1920; cover text inside the central 1080x1350 band (the
  Instagram grid crops to 3:4); nothing in the bottom 20% or the right 160 px.
- Source and risk lines go in the post caption, not on screen.
- At most one burned-in emoji (Apple-style or licensed), at the end of the hook, never in the brand's closing line.
- Text reacts; it does not explain.
- The look: the music-news house look (bold caps headline, the number in the accent colour, a tweet card over a clip,
  carousels) with the brand's accent colours (`music-news-pages.md` section 8). Not the TikTok white rounded box with
  black lowercase text ("TOOO off beat"), even though research said hits use it: the editor's taste wins on look.

### 9.3 The ad tells (measured on 22 of ours against 148 references)
- a logo or brand name on frame 0;
- template chrome: an avatar-and-handle header, category pills, slide counters, footers, legal lines;
- a designed card or dashboard in place of a moment;
- stock footage as wallpaper (the brand's own guide also banned stock photos);
- slide-ins, wipes or counting numbers;
- a pitch on screen ("Trade now", URLs, "zero fees", payouts);
- whoosh, tick or ding SFX packs;
- a new skin per format.
- Open tension (tentative): the house look carries a small profile-picture + @handle byline, while research counted an
  avatar-and-handle header as a tell on our designed cards. The editor chose the house look anyway; keep the byline
  small and watch the verdict on the first batch.

### 9.4 Real movement and one of two rhythms
- Pick one native rhythm per format: one continuous shot with one line (memes, fan moments, screen recordings: 20 of 49
  no-face references had no cut at all), or 3+ hard cuts per 10 s on the beat (edits: 17 of 49, up to 19.3). Ours sat in
  between (1.1 cuts per 10 s with designed frames changing every few seconds): "the worst of both".
- Motion comes from the footage or the live data line: 5% to 10% punch-ins on the number or the goal; hard cuts;
  something real moves in the first second; no 3D flips, no counting numbers.

### 9.5 Length: moments short, stories long
- 9 of the 12 highest view-to-follower ratios measured were under 20 s. Across 74 borrowed-footage hits the median was
  26.5 s: 33 at 20 s or less, plus a long cluster of 21 over 60 s.
- Memes and moments 6 to 20 s; story posts 25 to 60 s (a client creator guide: about 1 min; finance 40 to 60 s).
- A borrowed 16:9 clip can sit letterboxed on a black 9:16 canvas with the hook in the black space: 23 of 71 measured
  TikToks were not 9:16.

## 10. Sound like a page owner, not a template
- 130 of 148 native TikTok references ran on "original sound", 105 of them under the page's own name. "Fake sound" (our
  whoosh, tick and ding packs) was one of the 4 reasons the first video batch failed.
- Bake in the moment's own audio (the crowd, the commentary, our own screen recording), a voice, or a licensed track.
- Check every borrowed clip's sound: "original sound" under the uploader's own handle is usually real audio; a song
  title, or another account's "original sound", means music was laid on top (a spectrogram shows it): mute or replace
  it. When a clip carries someone else's song, build a scene bed from CC0 sounds instead of silence: a stadium bed at
  -12 dB with the crowd roar at -2 dB on the goal; a post-match crowd bed at -18 dB with camera shutters at -6 and -9
  dB; a match on a TV in the room at -16 dB; the mix normalised to about -14 LUFS.
- The account type decides the music plan: TikTok Business accounts see only the Commercial Music Library; many trending
  tracks are not cleared for business use; a commercial song baked into "original sound" is still unlicensed. Ask the
  client whether the accounts are Business or Creator. Tracks that match what works in the niche are named in the
  client's creator guide (a dark classical piece carried a 967K creator post).
- For gambling-adjacent brands: no casino or slot SFX and no "gamblecore" audio (its catchphrase says the banned word).

## 11. Footage for faceless posts
- Risk by footage type (format choice here; rights detail in `research-and-sourcing`): our own app recordings are the safe
  default; 2 to 3 s film or TV reaction clips are the least exposed; match broadcast is the most claimed (two of the
  biggest football edit pages show past bans in their handles); studios run their own clip accounts; a streamer's face
  next to a brand can read as an endorsement. Match, film and streamer clips are the client's decision.
- Keep a tagged reaction-clip library: 20 to 30 clips, 2 to 6 s each, green screen where possible. Moods: crying,
  shocked, stunned, smirk, laughing, devastated fan, frustrated manager, "calma", "this is fine". Why: reaction formats
  are the fastest daily posts, and the face says good or bad before anyone reads ("Owners rn" reactions did 395K).
- Stock is filler only (stock as wallpaper is an ad tell); frame-check every clip for faces and logos.
- News-agency and broadcaster photos are placeholders until licensed; flag them on the review page.
- Download goal clips from X the same day: the originals behind official-looking posts were already deleted (404) when
  fetched, and a broadcaster's post had only a thumbnail.

## 12. Honesty rules for money, trading and gambling-adjacent brands
- Keep supercar and luxury footage away from the product (tentative): a Lamborghini next to a trading app reads as "trade
  to get rich", and the client bans profit promises. A lifestyle POV keeps the call the viewer's ("pov: [match] tonight
  and you called [team] at 45%"), not a claimed trade. Note: the editor had listed supercar clips among acceptable
  footage types, so a supercar version was offered for comparison; the verdict was pending at 14 Oct.
- A "How did he know" smirk edit is honest only when a price really moved before the news; otherwise it implies insider
  knowledge, a legal risk (insider-timed trades drew Congressional letters in 2026). Log the time of the move and the
  time of the news. (A smirk edit did 43.1M on 145K followers in Jul 2026; ours used the smirk for "[team] fans who called
  it at 7%" instead.)
- Word swaps (the client bans "bet"; TikTok reads slips, odds boards and winnings as gambling cues; the brand guide bans
  payout percentages):

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| bet, bettors | call, took Yes or No, the money, traders |
| odds (in titles) | chance, priced at, the money says |
| parlay, slip, ticket | position, receipt (receipt only for a real position screen) |
| lock, banker | the money's favourite |
| cash out | sold at 8¢, paid $1 a share |
| fade, tail | with or against the money |
| "will happen" | "is priced at" |
| money glitch, free money, side hustle, gambling, casino, jackpot | drop entirely |

- Receipt-style posts go on X and Instagram only.
- Every number links to a live market with a date; real settled trades only; realistic money (one client: headline
  amounts under $10k, trades about $1,000 or less, the real event shown); losses as honest as wins; no profit promises;
  "Not financial advice" on finance.
- Refresh numbers on the posting day; "last night", "today" and "in 12 days" posts expire; check outside facts (a
  player's club, recent form) on the day.
- Promotion of these products falls under gambling or finance rules on every platform: check before any paid post
  (`platforms-and-policies.md` section 4). Some countries also ban making ads for online money games (an editor in one
  such country was flagged): `research-and-sourcing`.
- Never aim posts at countries the brand's terms block, and never hint at VPNs.

## 13. Clipping campaigns (if the agency runs or joins one)
- The leading prediction market funds public clipping campaigns (9 seen, run by an agency on a rewards marketplace).
  Per-1,000-view rates are in the source research, not in this kit (no prices in the kit).
- Brief rules seen: pages dedicated to one brand, on fresh accounts; 40% to 50% of views from the US or Tier 1 countries
  with 30-day bot checks; no reposts; clips at least 10 to 12 s; the logo near the bottom and never covered by app UI;
  "#Ad" plus the platform toggle; only countries the brand allows.

## 14. Review and hand-off
- One private review page per batch: posts with sound, each caption with a copy button and its posting window, a "why it
  works" line with the proof numbers and the source behind every number, grouped by format with the best evidence first
  and untested formats under "Tests" (build: `production-automation`).
- Placeholders until the client decides: page identity "@yourpage" with a dark "YP" avatar, the CTA in one config line,
  trader usernames masked; list what must change before posting (the real handle and avatar, fresh numbers on the day,
  the usernames OK).
