# 04 - Edit community (AMVs, anime / film / celebrity edits, AE + Alight Motion + CapCut culture)

Research date: 2026-10-13. Format follows `_brief.md`. No em dashes, no emojis (video titles are quoted without emojis).

## Read first: evidence key, limits, tool notes

- [S] = I looked at a contact sheet built from YouTube's own storyboard preview frames (image metadata, no video file downloaded). Frames are 1 per 0.7 to 2.4 s and between about 45x45 and 320x180 px, so I can see text, flashes, colour, glow, overlays and structure, but not sync to the frame. Where the sheet was tiny (long AMVs) I say "low-res". [M] = metadata only (title, description, tags, chapters, awards listed by the creator).
- "Peaks" = YouTube "most replayed" segments from yt-dlp (heatmap). Most peaks land right after the drop or on the last seconds (loop). Awards, project-file, "ib" (= inspired by) credits and software lines are quoted from the creators' own descriptions.
- YouTube numbers (views, likes, subs, dates) are from yt-dlp on 2026-10-13. Dates = upload date. Like rate = likes / views.
- TikTok cannot be read from this PC (yt-dlp connection refused, WebFetch refused). Instagram can: in my own browser-pane tab, profile pages show follower counts, tag grids such as https://www.instagram.com/popular/amv/ list top reels with play counts, and reel pages show likes, comments and date (no login, no clicking, read-only). IG numbers are from 2026-10-13. The shared web-search cap (200 per turn, all lanes) ran out about a third of the way through my run, so TikTok-native editors only appear with the evidence I saw (fan "best editor" lists, remake titles). No Reddit read (blocked).
- Biggest find for us: entries 53 to 55. An artist (Camie) released an album with a 21-minute AMV mix cut by about 50 credited editors, funded per-view pay for edits of each track on a marketplace (noisy.la, public rates on its page), and other editors then posted flow edits to her tracks (probably the paid campaigns, unverified). That is our business model already running at scale.
- Technique tags: [S] entries are tagged from what the sheet shows; [M] entries are tagged from the creator's text only. Where a tag comes from a tutorial about that creator's style rather than the video itself I mark it "(t)".
- Overlap: sanchezae's "Speed x Ronaldo" (2.48M views, 92.2k likes, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hnWplUUmWLQ) is analysed in `01-football.md` entry 1, so it is not repeated as an entry here. Hero Edit and aiyuk appear here for the voice-led intro structure and may also be in the football/basketball files.

Watch first (highest value for us): 53, 54, 55 (the artist-commissioned model), then 13, 20, 27, 31, 32, 33-35, 1, 3, 17.

## 4. Patterns noticed

1. Length and structure: the high-view YouTube edits are mostly 16 to 40 s (EPHIR 16 s 4.4M, Purgify 19 s 1.0M, Fictic Shorts 17-29 s 5.6-10.7M, Kung Fu Panda 21 s 5.5M). Many publish "Intro 0:00 / Edit 0:06-0:20" timestamps, so 30 to 40 percent is a quiet or story lead-in, then the drop. The replay-heatmap peaks sit right after the drop or on the last seconds (loop). Our 20.6 s with a 4.1 s build is inside this shape.
2. In the AE-style edits (not the contest mixes), type is the main differentiator, not cuts: almost every high performer puts lyric or quote words on screen (kinetic type, highlighted keyword, giant words behind the subject, 3D letters). The simple version (small subtitle lines with an accent word: Jemartob, aiyuk, orni.ae0) already reaches 0.5M to 6M views.
3. One identity per edit: a colour world (violet 9headache, teal Dr1zzy, section-coloured UPRISING), one overlay family (embers, lightning, light rays) or one prop family (clock and notebook in Death Note, a rendered subway station in Camie's Gojo edit) makes mixed sources look designed.
4. Effort is advertised and rewarded: titles with "10+ hours", "15+", "20 hours", "100+", "400+" run 4.2% to 6.6% like rate (Molob, Unique., zel, ZEORUZ, Hero Edit; n=9) versus 1.5% to 3.7% for plain-titled edits from the same scene (n=10). Correlation only. Effort per second of finished video: about 4 min (Hero Edit), 16 min (Molob Gojo), 35 min (Infinity Castle), 76 min (ZEORUZ), 290 min (Unique.).
5. Song-led: the audio is the thumbnail. Edits ride a specific edit-friendly version: "Slowed & Reverb", guitar remix, "Montagem ...", "Headlock" remix, NCS "Royalty", "TikTok ver.". Hit songs carry AMVs (Espresso 1.03M, How It's Done 1.86M, Dance The Night). For our client: ship edit-ready variants (slowed/sped, instrumental or guitar flip, a clean 20-30 s cut with a clear drop) and make the license simple.
6. "Edit audios" is its own discovery layer: compilation videos with timestamps of 30-60 s audio clips have 7.9M to 13.3M views (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UuebywLKWNM 13.3M, 2025-03-02; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l2AMLUZVzRo 9.0M, 2025-04-30; https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1eEMRl4Ervg 7.9M, 2024-08-20) and credit named audio-edit accounts. Getting the client's song into such lists is a promotion route in itself.
7. Two lineages: (a) contest AMVs reward concept, masking, clean content cuts and theme (Mob's Tea Party, Dance The Night, UPRISING) with little text; (b) TikTok/YouTube/Instagram "edits" reward hooks, type, flashes, shake and logo end cards. The 2025-26 edits we want sit between: contest-grade sync density in a 20 s hook-drop-loop shape.
8. Replay bait and a branded last second are both deliberate: hidden objects (monomogu), "count the references" (Babyartmusicpickle), loop tails (Unique., Fictic last-second peaks), search-bar or game-UI openers (montori, sanchezae); and a logo or name card in the last 1-3 s is near-universal among solo editors (Molob, Kuroroox, Flick, sanchezae, Fictic, aiyuk, Hero Edit). For us the song card plays the end-card role, and Camie's wording ("SONG PLAYING IS NOW WHAT ON ALL PLATS") is the clearest example.
9. Tool-agnostic: CapCut PC (Rethink:cc, 1.13M views), Alight Motion + Node Video (MKHL), a CapCut account with a 4.4M-play reel (celfstudies) and Premiere + AE + Audition (Babyartmusicpickle) all appear at the top. The craft is in the idea, type, sync and finishing.
10. Style diffusion is open and fast: top editors credit each other ("ib"), sell or give away project files (Molob 4,500+ asset pack; sanchezae 15 AE files at $21.99-$34.99; many free PFs), and others publish "edit like X" tutorials within months. Most-credited in my scan: GOJO (6 mentions across 3 editors), sanchezae (3), @pjunkie, @9headache, @uniquefx_, @blubzz (2 each). Several of the best are small on Instagram (montori 4.3K, Unique. 5.2K, Flimzy 2.2K) but strong on YouTube, so YouTube is the place to find them. A shared library of transitions, shakes and overlays is the realistic way to keep up.
11. Open collabs, MEPs and contests are the research labs: Blubz's 50K and 100K collabs (ranked lists of 22 and 28+ names), the 10-editor GOD-MODE MEP, creator-run AMV contests, and 20+ convention contests (Anime Expo, AnimeNYC, Otakon, Sakura-Con, Japan Expo, Anime Central, MomoCon and more) where the same editors win repeatedly. The anime-edit toolkit is already crossing to sports: sanchezae (Ronaldo x Speed), Dr1zzy (Blue Lock x World Cup, ib sanchezae), Hero Edit, aiyuk, Fictic.
12. The artist-commissions-the-edits model already runs at scale: Camie released an album on 2026-08-28, posted a 21-minute AMV mix with 36+ credited editors (367.9k views, 25.2k likes), per-track Instagram reels (EVOLVE: 108.4k likes) and ran 11 per-track campaigns on noisy.la (about $6.5k paid out at $0.65 to $1.45 per 1k views, caps $100-$150 per video); Noisy lists 45 song campaigns with typical rates of $1.00 to $1.50 per 1k views and pools of a few hundred dollars (Doja Cat $2.25/1k, $1k paid out). Other editors then posted flow edits to the tracks. Instagram volume for context: #amv 53M reels, #aftereffects 40M, #amvedit 12M, #animeamv 5.4M, #footballedit 2.5M, #movieedit 579K. This is both the competition and the pricing benchmark for the studio.
