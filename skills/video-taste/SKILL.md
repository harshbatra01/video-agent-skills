# Video taste, v1 (4 Nov 2026)

Claude cannot watch motion or hear sound. This taste comes from frame sheets, numbers and the user's verdicts. It is a starting opinion, and the user's eye overrules it. Every verdict from the user updates this file.

Evidence: skill `reference-study`, `examples/anthropic-ipo/LAST-VIDEO-RECAP.md`, skill `edit-loop-guard`.

## What I believe

1. **The world beats the graphic.** The best reference frames are real people in real rooms: a hearing, a studio, a TV set. A designed frame is the exception, so it feels like an event.
2. **Change the picture, keep the thought.** Altman's opening shows one man in about 7 clips, each in different light. It feels fast and is easy to follow. Elon's opening had about 5 subjects at the same speed.
3. **The image is the idea.** AC Elon's strongest frames are thoughts you can see: a talking head in an old TV, line-art evolution on "five years", a gun icon on cream. Choosing the image matters more than any effect on it.
4. **Contrast keeps the eye awake.** Dark, then white. Warm, then blue. Full frame, then one small object. Elon stayed dark and brown-gold, so nothing stood out. A stronger grade will not fix this. Picture choice will.
5. **Spend effects where the story turns.** A flash on the first second and on the reveal means something. A flash on every cut means nothing.
6. **Captions are quiet.** Small, centred, 1 to 3 words. The picture is the star.
7. **One finish holds it all together.** The same edge treatment over wildly different sources makes a montage feel like one film. It should be visible, but it is seasoning.
8. **Documents must be read at a glance.** One line, one highlight, clean. Asym holds attention with plain cards. Busy dark graphics lose it.
9. **Breathe on purpose.** One held shot or one quiet card after a run of fast cuts lands harder than another effect.
10. **Fewer sounds, each with a cause.** The viewer should feel the picture made the sound. Without ears, I choose from what listeners left behind: downloads, vote counts, descriptions. The user judges by ear.

## What I would push back on
- Dark frame, gold serif, money object: it reads as a luxury template.
- Any graphic the viewer must study in the first 5 s.
- An effect added because it is impressive in a demo.
- Stills filling space where a moving clip should exist.
- 3D or cute motion with no link to the story's tone.
- Copying the reference's most recognisable moves frame for frame.

## Questions before saying yes
1. What does a stranger understand in the first second of this shot?
2. Is the idea in the image, or only in the effect?
3. Is this different from the shots either side of it?
4. Remove it. Is anything lost?
5. Does it fit a serious story about real risk?

## From video-shotcraft's review rules (added 4 Nov 2026)
Another team's user notes reached the same faults as ours, which makes these stronger:
- Whole-frame hits at most 3 per video, others move only the element.
- Text is either readable (at least 5% of frame height) or blurred as texture. Never in between.
- A real-world sound of the action beats a generic whoosh. Pin each sound to a frame with a note on the action.
- Hold 1 s after key information lands.

## Limits
- Based on 4 references and 1 flop, with likes only and no retention data.
- Six frames of a motion demo do not show its easing, so motion quality stays unproven until the user sees a test.
