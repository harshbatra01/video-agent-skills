# The story-reel formula (measured)

The page studied is Attention Compound, a content agency that makes faceless narrated mini-docs (horizontal, posted as
reels). In October 2026 every script on the page was transcribed and coded by hand: 93 posts, 85 unique scripts, July
2025 to October 2026. The numbers below are medians of views read off the reels grid. Their scripts are paraphrased
here, never quoted; do the same in anything you publish.

This is reference data from another page, not from ours. Use it to choose a direction, then check the choice against
our own posts' results as they come in.

## The format
- About 81 s (38 to 120), 246 words (108 to 363), 191 wpm (141 to 229); voice from the first frame; about 4 s of music
  tail over the logo at the end.
- About 21 sentences of about 11 words; the first sentence about 12 words, finished by about 3.5 s.
- Per script (medians): 1 question, 1 sentence starting with "But", 1 with "Because", 2 numbers, 4 uses of "you", 2
  antitheses.

## What performed
| Factor | Median views |
|---|---|
| Sports topics | 113K (F1 alone 338K over 7 reels) |
| Every other topic (tech, finance, entertainment, consumer, luxury) | 5K to 9K |
| No business lesson (story only) | 170K |
| A business lesson at the end | 6.9K |
| Ending on a callback to the hook | 28.6K |
| Ending on an aphorism or one-line moral | 6.9K |
| Hook built on a number | 49.5K |
| Hook built on an origin story | 29.3K |
| Paradox hook (21 scripts) | 13.5K |
| Plain claim hook | 5.7K |
| Addressing "you" throughout | 318K (8 scripts) vs 7.5K addressing brands |

- The lesson gap holds inside topics (sports 172K vs 62K; tech 124K vs 8.8K), so it is not just the topic.
- Finance and VC topics never passed 16K.
- The top 15 reels: 40% had no lesson (6% of the rest), 73% had a news peg (43%), 73% used real clips (46%); they ran a
  little slower (181 vs 194 wpm) and a little longer (86 vs 80 s), with fewer "you"s and brand words.
- Same-day reposts sometimes beat the first upload; reposts days later never did (one Instagram page; our own Facebook
  data says the opposite for long videos, so check the platform). A real-clip cold open beat a spoken hook on the same
  story posted two minutes apart (7.6K vs 2.3K).
- They collect leads with comment keywords ("comment GROWTH") only on low-reach reels.

## The skeleton (positions as a share of the script)
1. **Hook (0 to ~3.5 s)**: about 12 words with a famous name or a number, usually a paradox or a cause and effect.
2. **Stakes**: why it matters, in one line.
3. **Open loop (~17%)**: the promise that something bigger is coming ("and that isn't even the strange part").
4. **Setup and proof**: dated scenes, real names, real numbers, real clips.
5. **The turn (~46%)**: a sentence starting with "But", or a one-word answer.
6. **Climax**: short beats, present tense.
7. **Lesson (from ~62%), only in lesson reels**; story reels skip it.
8. **Ending**: a callback that flips the hook, or a hypothetical aimed at the viewer.

## The cinematic devices that were wanted (story reels)
- A cause-and-effect paradox hook. A flat first version ("This summer, [a lab]'s own AI hacked another company, and
  [the lab] didn't even notice.") was rejected; the approved rewrite: "[The lab] gave its AI a test it couldn't pass.
  So the AI hacked a real company to steal the answers."
- A stakes line, then an open loop ("the hack isn't even the scary part").
- Date-stamped scenes ("May 2026."), present-tense narration, three-part fragments.
- The climax as one-word beats ("GO. With a six-minute deadline. It goes.").
- A rhetorical question as the twist; a kicker that calls back to the hook.

## A story, not a recap
A game-leak script written as news in date order was called "really boring" although every fact was right. The
rewrite:
- researched the backstory (an earlier leak years before) and the newest developments (the last 48 hours);
- opened on a concrete scene (a teenager in a hotel room hacking a studio with a hotel TV and a streaming stick);
- framed two acts from the hook ("stolen twice");
- planted a detail early (the leaker's own rule against selling the game) that paid off at the end (the leaker
  selling it);
- closed on a hypothetical aimed at the viewer;
- ran 356 words, just under 2 minutes (the long end of the range, accepted for a story with two acts).

## Writing for a mass audience
For a theme page, assume viewers know little about the subject: plain words, one everyday analogy (a reel about a model
that answers without generating text used "tea or coffee"), no jargon, story only. Leave out a company's best-case
claims (speed multiples from its own launch) unless a neutral source backs them, and say in chat what you left out.
