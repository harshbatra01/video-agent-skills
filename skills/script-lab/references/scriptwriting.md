# Scriptwriting

## 1. The core idea: expectation vs reality (C)
Viewers stay when what they get beats what they expected; they leave the moment it falls short. The hook sets the
expectation; every unit must beat it a little more. Rani's version: a new surprise every 10-12 seconds.

## 2. Pick a story structure (C), matched to our references
| Structure | Outline | Best hooks | Use it for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Listicle | Hook, 3-5 separate items, outro | List, problem | **Rani 4 cities and Gujarat.** Items that each stand alone |
| Breakdown | Hook, context shock, how it works, why it matters, outro | Secret reveal, question, comparison, contrarian | Launches, news, new tech |
| Newscaster | Hook, full context, a short take, outro | Secret reveal, question, comparison | A factual event |
| Case study explainer | Hook, the 2-4 part framework someone used, outro | Case study, question, comparison | How a brand got a result |
| Problem solver | Hook, problem (agitated), solution, outro | Problem, question, contrarian | Rani's lead posts |
| Tutorial | Hook, steps that build on each other, outro | Education | How-to |
| Personal story | Hook, the story start to end, outro | Personal experience | Founder origin, pinned intro |

## 3. The Rani skeleton (measured, all 11 reels)
Claim hook (0-4 s) > counted proof units, each with a number and a concrete noun > one turn line > CTA. 35-60 s,
100-170 words, no greeting. Full detail and the 30-check block list: `17-outlier-checklist-4-giants.md`.

## 4. The body
- **Order units so value climbs.** C: never let the second point be weaker than the first; he puts the second-best
  point first and the best second. Rani: the biggest gap goes last ("aur last", the 40x T-shirt). Our rule: open
  strong, climb, and save the biggest surprise for "and last".
- **The value loop per unit (C):** what it is (said as simply as possible), an example, and why it matters. In a
  12-second unit that is about one sentence each.
- **Rehook between units (C):** a short bridge that makes the next unit feel necessary. In a list reel the ordinals
  ("second", "and last") do most of this.
- **Zoom in, zoom out (C):** a concrete detail, then what it means, then the next detail.
- **Plain words (U, 20 Oct).** If a word needs explaining, swap it for the everyday word: "robots file" becomes "it
  blocks ChatGPT from reading its site"; "conversion" becomes "people were 3 times less likely to buy".

## 5. The turn and the ending
- One turn line that says what it all means for the viewer (R). A line people can quote and send (R shoes).
- C's outro: end on a high note that reminds them they got more than expected.
- **CTA as a native embed (C):** the resource is offered as the answer to the problem the video just showed, not
  as an ad. Rani's version: a keyword for a real resource ("Comment LIST"). The keyword is the same in the voice,
  the end card and the caption.

## 6. Emotion (C)
Write the one emotion you want at the top of the draft: awe, amusement, excitement, anger, surprise or curiosity,
sadness or empathy. Read the finished script and check you felt it. Rani's two winners: surprise (4 cities) and a
mix of amusement and mild guilt (cheap shoes).

## 7. Final four questions (C)
1. Is it interesting?
2. Is it as short as it can be?
3. Does the hook work on its own?
4. What did I feel at the end?
