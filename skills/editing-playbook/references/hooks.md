# Hooks

The first seconds decide whether anyone watches. Reels have no click-through rate: Instagram's skip rate (views that
leave inside 3 s) is the nearest thing, with 2026 averages of 60.5 to 65.5% by page size. Among 69 YouTube music edits,
41% had their most-replayed moment in the first 10%. So the hook gets the most care, and its length is set per format.

## 1. The hook the editor loved (cutdowns of long footage)
- 4 to 7 single-line beats taken from anywhere in the video, ordered **premise -> threat -> action -> an irony or twist
  closer**, cut to white on the last line. The story after it runs in source order.
- Each beat is one complete line whose picture shows it. The closer raises "how?" instead of showing the ending.
- It never shows injury, death or the final outcome: that spoils the payoff and risks a platform warning screen.
- Beats run 0.8 to 5.9 s, most 1.3 to 2.2 s, each an exact hold on that line's subtitle frames.
- Example, 6 beats in 11.4 s: "Scream for help! Get out of the car and scream for help!" / his kid: "Help!!!" / "You need
  to unlock the car" / "Or I will smash that window open" / "Disorderly conduct? A ticket?" / "It was gonna be a ticket,
  but now it's not".
- Verdict (14 Oct): "I really like the hooks of these last ones. You can be just a bit shorter". So the target became
  4 beats in 7 to 9 s (tighter beats, same order). A single-incident video can use one continuous moment of 3 to 4 s cut
  where it tips over ("much more curiosity invoking").

## 3. Rules that came from reviews
- **Curiosity, not a highlight reel.** A three-clip montage of "peak moments" was "so underwhelming"; never force a count.
- **Show the consequence and cut hard inside it.** The hook shows it getting away; dead beats inside the hook (a
  brake-light wait) go. Start on the word that matters: "cut it even shortere. directly when he says open the door".
- **A mid-story hook needs context:** a quick 2 to 3 beat premise first. Hooks carry their own setup (a quoted line
  with no context "feels out of place", friend's client).
- **Every beat shows something on screen.** A voice-only beat over nothing was dropped.
- **No pause over about 0.3 s inside a hook**, even inside one clip.
- **No separate hook when a short opens on action**: the opening line is the hook.
- **The beat right after the hook is a claim, a statistic or an unorthodox opinion, never an anecdote** (friend's rule
  for trailers): it tells the viewer what the piece is about while the hook still holds them.
- **Specific direct address beats generic** in ads ("if you signed up for a marathon this year", the boss).
- **Honest hooks:** a real number, a real poll result, a live conflict. Never an invented stat (declined when suggested).

## 4. Hooks by format
**News reels and native posts**
- Headline fully on screen from frame 0 (no fade, no logo intro), a face big, sound starting on the hook, the name and
  the conflict first. Frame 0 doubles as the grid cover: start where the face lands and shift the crop if it is cut off.
- Hook types that recur on winning pages: conflict with a clock ("reply drops Thursday 7pm"), "X just did Y" with a
  date, a surprising number, a curiosity gap paid off by second 6, the quote first then the name.
- Headlines 6 to 10 words, front-loaded; on-screen text at about 15 characters a second (minimum 1.5 s on screen).
- Urgency words (JUST DROPPED, OUT NOW, DROPS TONIGHT AT 7PM) only on time-sensitive stories; the rest lead with the
  fact and end on a question.
- Faceless: hooks are situations, shocks or a POV, never the market ("When your parlay keep losing by 1": 488K views on
  4,010 followers). 4 to 12 words, no greeting, no "odds", no venue name. Odds-titled countdowns got 0.22x their page's
  follower count in views against 10 to 105x for plain ones. Something real moves in the first second. Every number on
  frame 0 is final and true, never mid-count; motion renders start 0.5 s in.
- A real-clip cold open beat a spoken hook on the same story (7.6K against 2.3K, posted two minutes apart: friend's
  page).

**Ads**
- Open mid-action on the strongest image with a stakes line, never on the logo (research). A name or a number early.
- The talent's own authority line ("the three most important vitamins I used when I was a marathon runner"), not lines
  "an ai" could say.

**Explainers**
- A contrast hook ("$500 vs $30": price tells you nothing), then the presenter and "not sponsored", then the chapter map.

**Music edits**
- Frame 1 is already moving on the downbeat, one beat before the move; starting at the start of a play is "the most
  common amateur tell" (research). A quiet open beats a shouted one; a famous face in frame 1; the song's hook is the
  first visual hit; the song is audible from frame 0 (for a song promo the song is the product).
- Translate the hook and land it on a real gesture with the same meaning (a "so what happened?" taunt on an arms-wide
  shrug, the repeat on a hand to the ear).

**Trailers and cold opens (friend)**
- The single strongest moment opens the cut, cut before its own payoff so it still owes the viewer.

## 5. Where hook material comes from
- **Replay peaks.** YouTube's Most-replayed heatmap: the editor's own hook clips sat at a median 95th percentile of it
  (46 of 66 clips in the top 20%; 16 of 20 hooks used a top-5 peak). Pull it without downloading (premiere-ae-editing
  timing.md).
- **The payoff seen early, not shown whole:** the consequence, the threat, the twist line.
- **The song's most-replayed stretch** for music edits and news reels (start the window one beat before the drop).
- **The strongest still or headline** for faceless and news posts; the moment the number decided.
- **The talent's archive** for ads fronted by a famous person.

## 6. Testing hooks
- Trial Reels (Instagram, since Dec 2024) show a reel to non-followers first: A/B two openings of the day's best story
  where only the first 3 s change; check the toggle exists (a 1,000-follower minimum is reported, UNVERIFIED).
- Judge hooks by skip rate or 3-second view rate, not by likes. For cutdowns, compare hook styles across posts.
- Targets for a new Reels page: skip rate under 60% by week 4, then beat the page's own median (2026 averages: 65.5%
  at 1K to 5K followers, 63.4% at 10K to 50K, 60.5% at 100K to 1M); average reel watch time is about 8.5 s. Own medians
  come after about 20 posts (social-video-formats).
- Alternate hooks can live as separate sequences in the same project, each with a preview (friend's method), so the
  reviewer picks.
- Idea (tentative, not built): 10 s hook previews before a full render.

## 7. Never in a hook
- A logo or brand name on frame 0 (an ad tell on native pages).
- Injury, death or blood (spoils the payoff, risks a warning screen that stops recommendations).
- Notification-like blips in the first 3 s: TikTok TopView (the full-screen ad that opens the app) bans explosions,
  screams, phone rings and alarms there.
- A fake or unchecked number; a mid-count number on the cover.
- A beat with nothing on screen, a pause over 0.3 s, a line cut mid-word.
