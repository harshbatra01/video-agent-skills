# Cracked sports music edits, end to end

Sports clips cut to a client's song for music promo pages, packed with optical effects and synced to the beat. The song
is the product and must feel like the star. Learned 13-14 Oct 2026 on a pilot (eight versions, five reviews), a
frame-level study of about 30 top edits and three more edits. Taste verdicts in full: `effect-vocabulary-and-taste.md`
section 3. Scripting mechanics (beat detection, hit snapping, AE builds): premiere-ae-editing `references/timing.md`
("Music edits") and `references/after-effects.md` section 12. The project history and file locations: the client file
`~/VisionKit/kit/clients/cracked-sports-edits.md`. Why these edits sell songs (post titles, benchmarks, rights):
social-video-formats `references/sports-music-edits.md`.

## 0. The job and the bar
- The brief in the user's words: "CRACKED GOOD LOOKING FULL OF DIFF EFFECTS edits like crazy creativity... sync them to
  music beats and just juice them up".
- The bar: "we are the best in the world... absolutely crazy editors go so hard". Target = the consensus top of a
  research bank (edits that 2-4 researchers found independently). The user's own first edit is the starting point,
  not the bar.
- What separates the top from a plain edit: concept, designed type, compositing, hook and payoff, speed ramps, a loop
  or a live song-card ending. Flashes and shakes are table stakes. "Anything flashy is good. Anything crazy is good.
  Anything basic is bad."
- When he says the edit is not the focus ("The focus is training you"), deliver a training result: a frame study, a
  side-by-side proof sheet, a test reel and a density table, not another cut.

## 1. Order of work
1. **Study the reference at frame level.** Every frame (sheets of 60), not one per second: things happen for 1 frame.
   Diff all versions of a render to find the window that changed; read the effect stack from the project file (nests
   hide strobes and presets); measure sync, shots and structure. Write a breakdown: timeline, effect inventory, numbers,
   gaps.
2. **Build and read the research.** Parallel research lanes from one shared brief, consensus picks first. Then read ALL
   of it end to end (he noticed when only part was used: "go through the research once again properly"), filtered
   through the taste list, into one list of bases and ranked techniques. Note where research contradicts his verdicts.
3. **Choose subject and song.** A song that fits the subject; best if the athlete really used it (it cannot be faked
   and it moves the song). Get the audio, the kicks and snares, the word times and the song's most-replayed curve.
4. **Choose the base and the window** (sections 2 and 3). Plan the hook (frame 1 moving on the downbeat) and the
   payoff zone (55-70%).
5. **Footage** (section 14): catalogue, pick the second that matters from frame strips, check hidden cuts, burned-in
   captions, watermarks and bars.
6. **Plan.** Clips on the strongest hit, mattes and framing, the proven base set (section 7), the base's own devices,
   2-3 new techniques, hits (punch, shake, expo pop, silhouette), transitions, looks, overlays, SFX. Print the sync report.
7. **Preview framing without rendering:** every clip's crop at start, middle and end with its matte tinted, type boxes and
   the safe lines drawn. One sheet caught four framing problems in one round.
8. **Test any new effect first:** a test reel with one real cut per technique (1.2 s each); compare frame by frame with the
   reference (section 13) and tune per frame until the numbers follow the reference.
9. **Build** in Premiere (section 16): every clip, word, hit and transition a layer, nothing baked, with After Effects
   only making the effect elements (the old route was one live After Effects comp); test one moment; then the full
   H.264 preview; ProRes for the final.
10. **QA before showing** (section 13).
11. **Deliver:** answer his numbered notes one by one, give the render path, list on-screen facts to double-check and open
    asks. Keep every version under a new name; turn each verdict into a standing rule and a toolkit default.

## 2. Choose the base (structure library)
Measured on 22 reference edits plus research. Pick one base per edit for the song, the subject and the footage; mix
devices when they fit. Numbers are starting points.

| If the edit has... | Reach for |
|---|---|
| a hook or title naming a measurable idea (time, light, charge, count), or a sport prop (start lights, clock, scoreboard) | 1 Progress motif |
| a quote or interview that frames the player | 2 Quote-first tribute |
| a song-led promo (the song must stay audible) | 2 with the song's own intro lines as the "quote", or 1 + 5 |
| one iconic visual and a slowed or ethereal track, 12-18 s | 4 Single reveal |
| a history or era story, a big design budget | 3 Two-act graphic to real |
| a song that repeats a phrase or a drop | 6 Sequence callback |
| an 8-12 s hype loop | 7 Mograph strobe |
| lyrics that literally describe the footage, or two subjects (senior and junior) | 9 Lyric story / mirror |
| a fight sport | 10 Fight cold open |
| a song with a famous beat switch | 11 Bar build + beat-switch reset |
| one kind of moment across a career (celebrations) | 12 Theme compilation + loop |
| none of the above | 5 Steady pulse with type |

1. **Progress motif.** One prop from the subject's REAL world returns 3+ times and moves one step further each time,
   triggered by the same lyric; after the last step the edit releases into the payoff. Measured: start lights 2 / 4 / 5
   at 13.97, 17.31, 20.69 s, each 0.7-0.8 s, 3.3-3.4 s apart, a 0.3 s loudness breath before the first. Build 3-4
   inserts of 0.6-0.9 s spaced 3-4 s; release within one beat of the last state. A made-up prop was rejected as a
   gimmick. Variant that worked: **story in numbers**, real numbers huge behind him, one per strong hit on a lyric that
   earns it, each with a small caption saying what it is (birth year, home postcode, a record), facts checked.
2. **Quote-first tribute.** A spoken soundbite with keyword captions over a quiet beat, sparse cuts, a breath, then a
   dense montage (one reference: speech 0-15.6 s, montage from 16.5 s at 3-3.5 cuts/s). For a song promo use the song's
   own intro lines as the quote.
3. **Two-act: graphic to real.** A flat 2D act (silhouettes, colour blocks), the drop, then real footage with kinetic
   numerals.
4. **Single reveal / push-in.** Few shots (8 in 16 s), a slow push from a wide shot to an extreme close-up of the eyes by
   9-11 s, then a loop. Less is more when the reveal is strong.
5. **Steady pulse with type.** About 1 cut per second all the way, flashes spread evenly, giant words and composites every
   2-4 s, a 2-3 s outro card.
6. **Sequence callback.** A 5-shot sequence replays 9.8 s later when the music repeats, with heavier effects:
   recognition plus escalation makes the second drop hit harder.
7. **Mograph strobe.** About 3.8 cuts/s of designed poster frames (white, black, orange solids, collage cut-outs, a giant
   numeral); a 1 s pattern plays twice.
8. **Hook burst + slow section.** 7 cuts/s in the first 2 s, a long slow type-led middle, a montage at the end.
9. **Lyric story / mirror.** Two players mirrored, lyrics literal to the footage, few shots, a white flash, a logo end.
10. **Fight cold open.** 0-2.5 s a wordless B&W stare with a quiet name; a hard cut into colour on a snare; exchanges get
    shorter; the connecting punch on the lyric hit with impact frames; freeze on the reaction; end on the stare. "The
    silence is what people replay." Add a hit list (section 8).
11. **Bar build + beat-switch reset.** 0-2 s face; one play per beat-pair, escalating (arrival, handle, finish, absurd);
    the beat switch is the reset (poster-silhouette strobe, B&W, a quiet cinematic shot, the title); the new beat drops on
    its strongest hit with colour back and clips half as long; biggest play last, slowed, frozen, held past the line.
12. **Theme compilation + loop.** One kind of moment across the edit, the hook landing on a matching gesture; open and end
    inside the same shot so the loop seam disappears; the song title in the top bar.

More bases to try (research, not built): a 13 s duo loop (all hook), idol x famous fan (a second famous face in the first
second), velocity edit (every clip at 30-40% speed, ramping up 20-30 frames into each hit), end payoff (the strongest
image on the loudest hit in the last 2 s; only 3 of 69 edits peak at the end, a cheap A/B), a manager or legend persona
on UK rap, then vs now (B&W past, colour released on the drop), a ranked countdown (real data only), a meme template (a
1 s copyable action on the hook), a two-song switch, a skit or question hook, a world-swap metaphor.

Correction to the measured rule "a breath before the big moment: 0.3-0.6 s of dip or black": never a black frame. A
breath is a quiet shot, a freeze or an audio dip (he rejected the black frame).

## 3. The song and the window
- **Window:** start one beat before the drop, include the song's own most-replayed stretch on YouTube, end on a
  section's last downbeat plus one bar.
- **Song audible from frame 0** for a promo: no long spoken cold open over silence; run the song's intro under any quote
  or use a wordless stare. The song title goes in the post title; link the exact audio version.
- **Opening:** 41% of 69 YouTube edits have their most-replayed moment in the first 10%. Frame 1 is already moving on the
  downbeat, one beat before the move (starting at the start of a play is "the most common amateur tell"). A quiet open
  beats a shouted one; a famous face in frame 1; the song's hook is the first visual hit.
- **Payoff:** a dense 2-10 s zone at 55-70% of the runtime (replay data has two peaks: the first second and 55-70%), not
  one hit. Short edits under 20 s drop at 20-45%.
- **Lyrics:** lyric moments beat drum hits when the words matter (one edit put 13 of 24 cuts on strong drum hits by
  design and the rest on lyric words such as "hit"). Use the ONE lyric that matches the picture literally, on the real
  event: the real goal from the close replay, time-remapped so contact lands on a kick and the ball in the net on the
  biggest kick of the line. Translate a foreign hook and match a real gesture with the same meaning ("so what
  happened?" on an arms-wide shrug, the repeat on a hand to the ear).
- **Edit-ready audio:** ask clients for slowed + reverb, sped-up, instrumental and no-beat-switch versions and a clean
  20-30 s cut with a clear drop; edit pages ride these versions.

## 4. Sync
- Cut on the strongest kick or snare within +-0.2 s of each planned cut: score = strength x (1 - 0.5 x distance / 0.2),
  take the best, then FLOOR to a frame so the cut lands 0-1 frame early. A fixed beat grid scored at chance level (31% of
  cuts on strong hits, chance 27%); this got 25 of 26.
- Sync the transition's PEAK to the hit, not only the cut frame: blow-out white frames at -1/0/+1, the flash panels'
  fullest frame at 0 = the beat, strobe recipes starting on the hit. The strongest references' raw cut frames sync
  anywhere from 7% to 83%; their peaks sync.
- Hits land on the beat or slightly early (references: cuts -0.3 frames, flashes -0.5 to -0.75 frames), never late.
- Report sync before rendering: for each cut, the strongest hit 0-1.5 frames after it.
- Hit strength is scaled to the loudest hit in the window, so a song with a big level jump (a beat-switch drop) fails the
  report in its quiet part ("5/19 cuts on a strong hit", while most cuts sat within 1 frame of hits of strength
  0.13-0.35). Normalise strength per loudness section or per 4 bars before snapping and reporting (not built yet).
- Beat arithmetic: frames per beat = 60 / BPM x fps. 150 BPM at 30 fps = 12 frames (a 16th note = 3 frames); a slowed
  70 BPM = 25.7 frames. Tempo detectors read half or double time (one song measured 138, 94 and 70 BPM): trust the
  kick and snare list over the BPM.
- Snap every event to a frame (round(t x fps) / fps minus 0.1 ms); 4-decimal times put a third of all cuts one frame late.
- Title and lyric words use word times floored to a frame; cuts use the strongest hit.
- "Kick = cut, snare = flash, 808 = shake" has no source behind it: folklore, test by ear.

## 5. Density and rhythm
- **Effect-state density** (measured per 10 s on the picture band only; crop the letterbox bars first or every white
  frame is hidden): the top three references had 108-116 frames in a blur or transition state and 6.3-10.8 white frames;
  our plain cut-and-punch version had 3.4 and 0.6 (about 1% of frames active, which read as "basic"); the version with
  an optical transition on every cut had 27.8 and 10.6.
- **Targets:** 20-35% of frames in a transition or effect state, a transition on every cut, about 5 white frames per 10 s,
  25+ colour bursts per 10 s.
- **Rhythm:** "flashy" does not mean more cuts. Premium football edits cut 0.6-1.3 times a second and 40-75% of their
  shots last 3/4 to 3 beats; the flashiest hold 2-4 beat shots (0.6 cuts/s) and fill them with ramps, glows and flicker.
  Mograph strobe edits are the exception: 3.8 cuts/s, 84% of shots under 3/8 of a beat.
- **Vary density by section:** a flash and a shake on every beat is the average, not the top. One top edit has 12
  near-white flashes in about 20 s, densest in the drop; another uses only 2 in 30 s and leans on type. The densest
  stretch is the payoff zone.
- For comparison, the user's own first edit: 26 shots in 20.6 s (median 0.62 s), about 1.2 flash or strobe events per
  second, 65% of big frame changes within 1.5 frames of a kick, snare or onset, the drop at 20%.

## 6. Layout: the band and the layer order
- **The band:** the picture in a 1080x800 band on the 1080x1920 frame (y 560 to 1360), black bars above and below. Why:
  we see more, the virtual camera moves less, and type gets room. He asked for it after full-frame 9:16 versions. A band
  layout is fine on Reels (top edits post widescreen inside the vertical feed).
- **Framing:** from a matte of the subject, not a person-detector box (box trackers jump to whoever is largest). Head and
  shoulders = the centre of the top 45% of the shape, smoothed and speed-capped; subject at about 55% of frame height,
  zoom capped at 1.5-1.8x. Keep tracking calm: a janky first frame was the first note. Everything in frame.
- **Type safe zone:** 10% to 76% of the frame height.
- **Layer order** (bottom to top): footage; type behind the player + the player's cut-out; silhouette strobes; section
  looks; the grade and vignette; hit adjustment layers (punch, shake, zoom cut); the bar solids; type that sits in the
  bars; pop-out cut-outs (the player breaking over the bar edge); front type; flashes and transition frames. Why: a
  vignette or grade over a flash turns flat white into a grey gradient (the training round moved them below the
  transitions; an earlier order had the grade on top). Flashes are band-size solids: a part-opacity full-frame white
  turns the black bars grey and looks cheap. A flash also hides a white silhouette strobe on the same frame: use one or
  the other.
- **Mirror extension:** every footage layer gets Motion Tile (output height about 170%, mirror edges) so distortions never
  pull black in from outside the band. Never on an adjustment layer (it squashes the whole comp into the tile). Pop-out
  layers above the bars must not carry it, or a mirrored copy of the head shows above the band.
- **Footage audio off** on every footage layer and every duplicate (clip audio mixed into the song in four renders; the
  user called it "A MAJOR BUG").

## 7. The proven base set (in every edit)
### 7.1 Number or word behind the player (the effect he loved most)
- Font: an ultra-heavy grotesque (Galano Grotesque Black) for numbers, a heavy condensed face (Coolvetica Heavy Comp)
  for names; 400-560 px (760 px for a name).
- Place it from his matte: x from his head in comp pixels; the glyphs' centre near the top of his head (lifted 0-0.3 x
  glyph height), so the tops of all digits show and his head covers only the lower middle. Keep it 12 px inside the band
  and 30 px from the frame edge; centre it when it is wider than 1000 px.
- It slams in from 128% with a coloured depth copy behind (garnet, grey or red). On its first frame: a white silhouette
  of him (frame 0, glow 45) plus Exposure +0.5 for 0.18 s.
- Caption in the bottom bar at y 1402 (34 px), an optional second line 48 px lower (26 px, tracking 300), saying what the
  number is. A bare number means nothing.
- Impact SFX 5 ms early at -21 dB under the song.
- One number per strong hit, on a lyric that earns it; every fact double-checked before posting; never a fake stat.
- A thin gold rim on the cut-out is fine here (matte grown with Minimax 7, glow 45, gold [1, 0.78, 0.15]); a glowing aura
  is not.

### 7.2 Title in the top bar, word by word, his head popping out over it
- Coolvetica Heavy Comp sized to fit 990 px wide (real font metrics), word gap 0.22 em, baseline at y 540 (just above the
  band's top edge at 560), a red depth copy [0.78, 0.06, 0.12], each word slamming from 135% on its sung time (floored to
  a frame).
- For the title's span the clip's cut-out is laid again ABOVE the bars, with no mirrored band extension. Verdict: "this
  is good". Why it works: the black bar becomes designed space.

### 7.3 Lyric words in the bottom bar
- Coolvetica Heavy Comp 96 px, shade 0.6, slam from 140%, y 1446, word times from the transcript floored to frames.
- Only the lyric that matches the picture becomes the payoff caption (a line about taking one shot and hitting the target, on the real goal). The song stays the hero.

### 7.4 White silhouette strobes and the inverse strobe
- One frame of the player as a white silhouette, on 2-3 frames with 1-frame gaps around a hit.
- Inverse: the background flashes white and the player stays.
- In After Effects: a band-size solid track-matted (luma) by the clip's matte. In Premiere: section 15.

### 7.5 Flash panels (his "geometry")
- The picture band is split into n panels (columns or rows). Each frame lights a set of panels at 100% with hold keys and
  crisp edges. The fullest frame is frame 0 = the beat. Panels still lit at the end fade over 4 frames into the next shot.
- Patterns: `fill` (one more panel per frame, starting n-1 frames before the beat, 4-frame fade), `fill_rev`, `fill2` (two
  per frame), `centre` (middle outwards), `alt` (odd, even, all), `pass` (a lit block passes over the cut), `unfill`
  (full on the beat, panels switch off one by one to reveal the shot).
- Used: 7 vertical white bars `fill` n=7; cream halves `pass` n=2 rows, colour [0.94, 0.94, 0.88]; 5 horizontal white
  bands `fill` n=5 into a chapter; `unfill` n=7 two frames after a type flash; `centre` n=7 cream.
- His own description of his halves: "bottom half of the screen first, then bottom and top half, and then just top half,
  and then nothing". The built `pass` lights the top first: confirm against his frames and flip if needed (open).
- Why: it is the one geometric family he asked for, and it lands frame by frame like his own edit. Spins, slides, box
  glitches and blades are the "PowerPoint" geometry he rejected.

### 7.6 The strobe letter code (one character per frame)
A top transition is often a row of distinct 1-frame looks; writing it as a string makes it easy to plan, vary and sync.
All band-size, 1 frame each:

| Letter | Look |
|---|---|
| E | line art on white (Exposure +1.2, contrast 45, Find Edges, B&W, Levels in-black 0.2 / in-white 0.78) |
| K | glowing lines on black (E + Invert, Levels 0.3 / 0.9, optional Tint, glow 30 / 18 / 0.6) |
| I | colour negative |
| N | mono negative |
| B | hard B&W (contrast 70) |
| W / X | white frame / black frame |
| S / H | bright vertical / horizontal streak (Motion Blur 300 px, Transform scale 180 on one axis, Exposure +3.5, offset 0.22, B&W) |
| D | dark streak (Exposure -6.5) |
| C | colour streak (Exposure +2.3, Lumetri saturation 220, contrast 55) |
| Z | zoom-blur burst (scale 130, CC Radial Fast Blur 90, Exposure +0.5) |
| T | impact frame (Threshold 0.43, the script value for 110/255) |
| V | inverted impact frame (T + Invert) |
| R Y U G P O | colour solids at 86% with the picture ghosting through (red, yellow, blue, green, purple, orange) |
| . | the shot untouched |

Recipes in use: "EKEK" (gold lines [1, 0.85, 0.3]), "EKEKSD" into a B&W chapter, "SDSDCC", "EKE" on a strike, "VT" on
contact frames, "I" and "I.I" flickers, "N" inside B&W. Black frames (X) are rejected on their own as breaths: use them
only inside a strobe. Long runs of colour letters become a flood: keep them short.

### 7.7 Silhouette strobe stack codes
Per frame: F = flat field colour with the next shot's player as a white silhouette; I = white field with the player in
the field colour; K = black with a white player; W = white; P = white field with a BLACK player (the poster frame). Field
colour maroon [0.62, 0.05, 0.18]. "WFIK" was kept; "PK" and "PPK" are not judged yet. Built as band solids plus solids
luma-matted by the clip's matte.

### 7.8 An optical transition on every cut
Light blow-out, zoom rush with a radial chromatic split, line-art strobes, invert flickers, true RGB split, defocus, short
vertical smears, a negative strobe into a B&W section with 12 fps Posterize Time inside. Status of each and the rejected
ones: `transitions.md`; frame recipes and build facts: premiere-ae-editing `references/transitions.md`.

## 8. More devices (built, not judged yet)
- **Type flash:** the flash carries a lyric word. Per frame W = a black word on a white band, B = a white word on a black
  band; pattern "WB"; Coolvetica Heavy Comp 290-420 px fitted to the band. (From a lyric video with 8.5M views.)
- **Numeral sandwich:** a hollow numeral behind (outline 760 px, stroke 8, gold [1, 0.82, 0.3]), a heavy word in front
  (220 px with a navy depth copy), a small tracked label in the bottom bar. Place the numeral on the side with more room:
  a centred narrow "1" behind a centred player vanished.
- **Hit list:** real names struck through one per hit (taken from the footage's own captions): Proxima Nova Extrabold
  46 px, tracking 260, a red bar [0.9, 0.06, 0.1] 16% of the type size thick wipes across in 2 frames on the strike, the
  name dims to 55% over 3 frames. A lyric about ticking them off became a story device.
- **Ghost trail:** 6 cut-out copies, copy i delayed by i x 0.05 s, opacity 70 x 0.78^i, Tint 70%, Exposure +0.8, a live
  cut-out on top; all snap off at the end time. Motion trails are common in sports ads and rare in short edits.
- **Pop-up cut-out freeze:** freeze 8-14 frames; the frozen background blurs 0 -> 26 and darkens (Exposure 0 -> -1.1) over
  3 frames; the cut-out scales 100 -> 114 -> 110% about his head over 4 frames with a soft drop shadow; white frames at 90%
  then 35% on frames 0-1. A current trend; fits a breath before the hook.
- **Freeze to photo print:** a cream paper band solid [0.93, 0.92, 0.88]; the held frame masked with 8% side and 9% top
  margins; rotation 0 -> -2.5 deg and scale 108 -> 100% over 3 frames; a drop shadow; a label in Proxima Nova Semibold
  30 px, tracking 340; one white frame at 95%; about 12-13 frames long. It echoes the cream panels he likes.
- **Stutter strike:** the contact frame repeated in 1-3 frame bursts over a snare roll (play, jump back, repeat, keys on
  exact frame times, no frame blending on that clip); once per edit.
- **Freeze clone:** across one bar a frozen copy of the cut-out stays on each beat; all vanish on the white strobe at the
  downbeat. Frozen clones only read on a static camera (a broadcast camera follows the player, so the copy lands on him).
- **Impact frames:** on the contact frame, 1 frame of hard B&W threshold inverted (black player on white), then 1 frame
  normal threshold, then colour (from anime impact frames). Letters T and V above.
- **Flash-on-word:** 1-2 frame exposure pops on the sung syllables, so the song becomes the hero.
- **Giant name behind him:** 2-3x the frame width, cropped by the frame edges; or a type wall (rows of the name sliding
  opposite ways behind him).
- **Velocity ramps anchored on the beat:** a ramp only reads if the source frame of the skill lands exactly on the hit. A
  Gaussian speed dip around the anchor: far speed 1.1-1.4, near speed 0.35-0.4, width 0.10-0.14 s, keys every 1/15 s,
  source time pinned to the anchor. Research rule: base 30-40% speed, ramp 20-30 frames into and out of each hit, slow on
  the wind-up, real time on contact.
- **Colour pop** only on a shot with visible background (an adjustment layer at saturation 0, contrast 25, the colour
  cut-out laid above it): on a tight close-up there is nothing to turn grey.

## 9. Type for music edits
- One typeface family plus one accent colour per edit. Premium faces: ultra-condensed heavy grotesque (Druk / Anton
  class), wide heavy italic, high-contrast serif, script signatures. A giant surname 2-3x the frame width cropped by the
  frame edge; fill on the kick, outline on the snare.
- Avoid default caption templates (Montserrat plus a yellow keyword plus an emoji), type on the face or body, a new font
  every shot.
- Type is designed and textured (depth copy, grain, sheen): big word plus small caption, not spaced out over the face, not
  bland. Text only where it means something.
- Lay type out with real font metrics (a condensed face is about 0.4 em per letter, a wide black face about 0.6) and plan
  in baselines: capitals sit about 0.67-0.72 x size above the baseline, so a plan that treats y as the centre puts big
  words a third of their size too high.
- Textured type with no plug-in: Gradient Ramp on the text layer (points in the layer's own space), Noise about 14% inside
  the letters, a soft Glow, and an offset copy in a colour behind it that slides out 3% of the size on the slam (printed
  depth). Mechanics: `titles-captions-type.md` section 2.
- The bank's numbers: kinetic type is the most used technique in top edits (98 of 273), then colour grade (72), glow (60),
  beat cuts (57) and lyric text (54). Typography and text behind the subject are "the premium tell".

## 10. The look
- A fixed, light house look so broadcast footage stays natural and the flashes do the work: Lumetri contrast 18,
  saturation 92 (scripts use 0-300 with 100 = normal), temperature -4, highlights -12, shadows -6, blacks -8; vignette 28;
  grain 3; bloom threshold 86, radius 60, intensity 0.16. A soft whole-picture bloom had turned the pitch neon until its
  threshold went up.
- A B&W chapter: saturation 0, contrast 40, exposure -0.4 with 12 fps Posterize Time; colour returns through a light
  blow-out. Keep 1-frame flickers above the Posterize Time layer or they smear across 2-3 frames.
- Colour as structure (research): one colour or overlay family per edit; B&W or a tint for a quiet bar and colour back on
  the drop; change the flash colour per hit; end on a B&W card; then vs now as a grade.

## 11. Sound
The song is the product; SFX only add weight to cuts. One procedural SFX family per edit, mixed 11-25 dB under the song
(no audio notes came back on these levels):

| Event | Placement | Level |
|---|---|---|
| whoosh up into the drop | 0.36 s before the drop | -15 dB |
| impact on the drop | 5 ms before | -13 dB |
| short whoosh before each optical transition | 0.15 s before | -20 dB |
| glitch before a strobe recipe | 0.03 s before | -25 dB |
| short whoosh before flash panels | 0.2 s before | -20 dB |
| impact before each number slam | 5 ms before | -21 dB |
| riser into the strike | 1.2 s before | -21 dB |
| impact on the ball in the net | on the frame | -11 dB |

- Built silence (research, untested): drop the music 5 dB with the treble cut for 4 or 8 beats before a big hit; audio
  off for 2 frames at a beat switch; on fight endings take the music out a beat early over room tone.
- Check every render for footage audio: subtract the song after cross-correlating for the encoder delay (AAC adds about
  21 ms); correlation should be about 0.99 and the leftover far below the song (a good render: 27 dB under).

## 12. Endings and loops
- End on the phrase's last downbeat and hold an end card for one more bar while the music plays on, fading the last
  0.6 s. Stopping 0.1 s after the next section's first downbeat felt "cut off".
- Biggest play last, freeze on the reaction, hold a beat past the music (or take the music out a beat early).
- End card for a wide gesture that a 9:16 crop would cut (arms wide): the clip at "contain" scale in the middle over a
  cover-scaled copy of itself with Fast Box Blur 40 and Exposure -1.6, a slow push 100 -> 105%, the title in the dark band
  above and a credit below. The user's own edits end on a song card (a streaming-player card) instead; the song card plays
  the end-card role for promos.
- Loops drive rewatches: open and end inside the same shot so the seam disappears (the end plays toward the opening
  frame). Make a 13 s loop version of every edit (research rule, not tested yet).

## 13. QA before showing
1-frame events and sync slips are invisible at normal speed. Before showing any version:
- Measure the render: sync of cuts on strong hits against chance; the effect density per 10 s against the references.
- Every changed moment as labelled QA windows at full size, plus one pass over the whole render every third of a second.
- The audio subtraction test (section 11).
- The framing preview before rendering (section 1 step 7).
- For a new effect, compare ours and the reference side by side at the same height, frame by frame, with three numbers per
  frame: L = mean luma, S = luma spread (a flat white or black frame has S under 8), C = colourfulness (mean of max-min
  RGB). The numbers find what the eye glosses over. Flat white at most 3 frames per transition.

## 14. Footage
- Hygiene: rename files to ASCII (emoji names broke tools); find the real picture area inside pillar or letter bars from
  the median frame; crop burned-in captions and score bugs with a zoom (about 1.25-1.6x on a band); frame a watermarked
  pack low so the band starts below the mark.
- Pick the second that matters from 8-frame strips per shot (a shot showed shins instead of the ball).
- Score shots for how clearly the subject is in them: about a third of a fan pack's "action" shots hide him.
- Use the real angles of a key moment in real time: one wide shot zoomed 2.2x was "too small to read".
- Source quality is part of the look: heavy effects on 720p look cheap; ask the user for 4K packs when downloads are
  blocked.
- Check hidden cuts inside chosen source ranges of compilations and scene packs (replays, angle changes).
- Subject re-ID and tag prompts are sport- and kit-specific: give a hand box at a named time when the matte picks the
  wrong person.
- Sourcing, rights and download routes: research-and-sourcing.

## 16. Premiere or After Effects for these edits (decided 15 Oct 2026)
Music edits are assembled in Premiere (the boss works in Premiere); After Effects only makes the effect elements
(alpha overlays and plates). The earlier edits were built as one live After Effects comp (every clip, word and hit a
layer, a change = one layer edit and one render), which kept the build-in-Premiere rule's purpose but not its letter.
That single-comp build, wherever this file or the music-edit kit (section 18) describes it, is the old route.

## 17. Next techniques, with build notes and taste risk
- Depth-map strobe or fog: 1-2 frames of a grey depth map on the snare (needs a free depth model and the user's OK to
  install).
- Material strobe: only the cut-out turns halftone or hatched for 2-3 frames.
- 3D extruded word behind him (Blender); world swap behind the cut-out for 0.5-1 s per drop.
- Rays shake: CC Light Rays on the head 0 -> 250 -> 60 plus a +-40 px, +-2 deg jolt over 8 frames.
- Anamorphic streak: an 8 px white solid, Fast Box Blur 250 horizontal, Add, swept in 4-6 frames.
- Pose-match flash cut: 1 white frame, then Camera Lens Blur 50 -> 0 and scale 108 -> 100 over 6 frames.
- Glass or hex refraction (CC HexTile, 6-10 frames); masked soft twitch (only the player jolts, 2-8 frames); halftone
  ripple; time slice (keep it under 12 frames, risk: "blades"); glass shatter (studied, not built).
- Research claims to test before relying on them: Premiere 26 Object Mask and AE 26.2 Object Matte (one-click subject
  mattes); Film Impact transitions free in Premiere; Animation Composer.
- Each one gets a test reel and a frame check against the verdicts before it goes in an edit.

one plan, so the next edit gets the fix. The kit ships in the kit library: `~/VisionKit/kit/library/pipelines/edits-kit/`
on the boss's Mac (`tools/`, `tools/ae/`, plus `STRUCTURES.md`, `STUDY.md`, `RESEARCH_V2.md`); run order and Mac
