# The mass reel formula

Built from Rani's 11 reels (measured), Cutroom's reel and hook research (measured on other pages), the Callaway
scripting method (practitioner) and our own review rounds. Where they disagree, the Rani data wins for this kind of
reel (a founder talking head going for mass reach), and the user's verdicts win over everything.

## 1. The ten laws

1. **Reveal, don't assign homework.** Tell people something true about the world they didn't know: places, prices,
   what a famous company did. Instructions come at the end, once, if at all. (H, I, K: zero instructions.)
2. **Widest rung that still fits the lane.** Everyone > shoppers and would-be sellers > would-be founders > founders >
   a niche. Pick the highest rung your fact can honestly serve.
3. **One big, simple gap.** A price gap ("cheaper than China"), a belief flip ("you think you saved"), a secret ("they
   blocked it"). Said in the first breath.
4. **Things people can picture.** Named places, brands, objects. Each name is a comment thread ("you forgot my city").
5. **A counted shape.** First, second, third, and last. "And last" keeps people to the end and holds the biggest gap.
6. **One simple number per unit.** A price, a count, a multiple. Never chain numbers into maths. Keep density at
   about 3-6 numbers per 100 words.
7. **Talk like a friend.** "You think you saved money" beats "you should". No lecturing, no "but actually".
8. **Ask almost nothing.** Know it, save it, send it, buy smarter. Never "spend 2 weeks doing X".
9. **Keep every promise.** The title band, the hook, the body and the caption say the same thing. The number in the
   hook is the number the body proves.
10. **End on something people keep.** A list worth saving, or a line worth quoting. Then one CTA.

## 2. The six formats (topic-free templates)

### Format 1: The place list (H, I) — the strongest
- Hook: "In these [N] [places], you get [what everyone wants] [better than the rival you know]."
- Optional personal line (I): "I found out when I [did the real thing]."
- Units: "First, [famous place]. [What it's known for, or a flip on what it's famous for]. [One number]."
- "And last, [place]": the biggest gap.
- End: "Want the full list? Comment [WORD]."

### Format 2: The "you" belief flip (F)
- Hook: "[Everyday thing you do], and you think [belief]."
- A tiny story: you vs a friend, months later.
- Escalate to 2-3 more everyday cases.
- A quotable moral, then "follow" (no lead magnet).

### Format 3: The famous-brand secret (A)
- Hook: "[Famous brands] do one simple thing before [moment]."
- The method in 2-3 short steps, one number.
- What not to do. CTA keyword for the exact process.
- Ceiling: about 3x median here, because the method is familiar advice.

### Format 4: The rival-markup reveal (E)
- Hook: "[Our thing] is cheaper than [rival's], but [someone else] sells it back at [N]x."
- The injustice line ("our crop, their brand").
- Works only on a subject most viewers already know.

### Format 5: The A/B quiz (screenshots)
- Hook: a scenario with A and B. A countdown. The reveal with one reason. The rule in one line.
- The comments are the answers.

### Format 6: Advice and blueprints (B, D, G, J) — the baseline
- Low-capital blueprints, management lessons, "test before you build".
- These hold the median; they don't travel. Use them for lead posts, or turn them into a Format 1 list (places,
  tools, companies) so the subject becomes something you can picture.

## 3. Unit grammar (one unit = one 10-14 second beat)
`[Ordinal] + [Famous name] + [What it's known for / the flip] + [One number] + [What it means for you, in 5 words or
none]`

Example (H): "Teesra hai Kannauj [ordinal + name], jise India ka Perfume capital kaha jaata hai [what it's known
for]... sirf Rs 15-20 mein 100 ml itr [one number]."

## 4. Hook patterns that fit this account type
| Pattern | Rani example | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "In these N places you get X better than [rival]" | H, I | You have a list of named places or companies |
| "[Habit], and you think [belief]" | F | An everyday behaviour everyone has |
| "Do you know [famous brands] do [thing]?" | A | A real practice of known brands |
| "This [thing] is cheaper than [rival], but [twist]" | E | A markup or arbitrage everyone can picture |
| "[A] or [B]? Which one?" | Quiz | A choice with one clear answer |
| Avoid: "Just Rs X can make you Rs Y" | C | Only if the body proves Y exactly |
| Avoid: "The biggest achievement in business is X" | B | Plain claims hold the median |

Practitioner list (no view data): 26 hook formulas from Jakeschincariol/instagram-agent-skill, saved in the job at
`research/hooks-26-jakeschincariol.json`. Fits our data: Negative Command (hook only), Wrong Way Right Way, Cold Open
Demo (real footage only), Numbered With A Favourite. Avoid: Nobody Tells You when the source is public, The Statistic
without a real number, The Superlative, Insider Leak or Cost Confession without the person's real numbers.

Cutroom's measured page found number hooks beat plain claims (49.5K vs 5.7K median); Rani's data shows the gap and
the rival matter more than the number. Use a number in the hook when it IS the gap ("Rs 500 shoes").

## 5. Language and delivery
- Plain words a 14-year-old knows. Technical names only on screen.
- Long spoken chains joined by "and", one image per clause, the way people talk. Read it aloud; if you run out of
  breath, split it.
- About 2.7-3 words a second in English (Rani: about 3.4 in Hinglish). 35-60 seconds.
- Informal second person: "you think", "your friend". Avoid stacks of "you should", "you need to", "you can".
- No "but actually" arguments; let the payoff turn the story.
- No greeting, no intro, no "in this video".

## 6. Endings and CTAs
- Reach post: a quotable line, then follow or a question anyone can answer.
- Lead post: "Comment [WORD]" for a real resource that exists before posting; the same word in the voice, the end card
  and the caption.
- Never two spoken CTAs.

## 7. The series engine
A winner becomes a franchise within days: the same formula, a new place or a new company (H, then I, then K).
Recut winners as variants, but not to the same audience the next day.

## 8. Truth rules (non-negotiable)
- Every number and claim checked at the source on the day you use it. A fact pulled by a script is opened and read
  before use (a bot-protection page is not data).
- No superlatives you can't source. No motives the source doesn't state. "Can" when the source says "may".
- The AI avatar never says "I tried it" about something the person didn't do.
