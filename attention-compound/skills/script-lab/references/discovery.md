# Discovery: finding a topic that can travel

## 1. Start on the widest rung (Rani's audience ladder)
Views fell almost exactly down this ladder on Rani's page: everyone (cheap shoes, 853k) > shoppers and would-be
sellers (4 cities, 2.6M) > would-be founders (21-94k) > existing founders (24-36k) > founders with one specific asset
(WhatsApp list, 6.6k). For an expert channel the rungs are:
1. Anyone who shops or uses AI.
2. Anyone who sells online.
3. The ICP (for one client: US merchants with big catalogs).

Reach posts start on rung 1 or 2 and earn the right to be useful to rung 3. Lead posts can sit on rung 3.

## 2. Ask what the wide audience already asks
- Google autocomplete with broad seeds ("why is temu so cheap", "can chatgpt buy things for you"):
  `research/broad/suggest_broad.py`.
- Reddit, Quora and community threads for the niche questions (`07-demand-scan.md`).
- The outliers in the niche: which topics and which hook formats keep winning (Callaway: build a watch list, sort by
  outlier score, look for the pattern in 20-30 top videos).

## 3. Wrap it around names everyone knows
Rani's winners all hang on something the viewer already knows: China, Agra and the Taj Mahal, Mamaearth, shoes. For
our lane: Amazon, Walmart, Google, ChatGPT, Shopify, Temu, Nike.

## 4. Find the facts with the most shock (Callaway's law of interesting)
- **Shock = the distance between what the viewer already knows and what you tell them.**
- **Shock score:** for each fact, out of 100 people like the viewer, how many have NOT heard it? 80 = a fact 4 in 5
  people don't know.
- Gather 5-10 true facts, score each, and build the outline only from the high scorers. A fact under about 50 is
  background, not a unit.
- Every fact still has to pass our truth rules: a checked source, no superlatives we can't source.

## 5. Check the idea before writing (the gate)
- Breadth-Gap-Proof test (`research/reference-rani/formula-breakdown.md` section 5): breadth, gap, anchor, proof,
  identity, lived line, 0-2 each; build at 8+ of 12.
- Callaway's outline test: are the points actually new, or a summary of what everyone knows? If they're not new, go
  back and research. "Measure twice, cut once."
- Series potential: can it become Part 2 (Rani's cities, then Gujarat)?
