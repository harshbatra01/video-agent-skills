# Hooks

Every hook rule we use, with where it came from. Sources: **R** Rani (measured), **CR** Cutroom (measured where
noted), **C** Callaway (practitioner), **U** our own verdicts.

## 1. The 9 hook formats (C), and where our references sit
| Format | One line | Example shape | Seen in our references |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Secret reveal | You know something the viewer doesn't | "[Big name] just released [X] that makes [Y] faster" | Rani "Gujarat's hidden markets" |
| Case study | A brand or person got a result in an unexpected way | "[Name] did [result] in [time] using [method]" | Rani brand-test reel (Mamaearth, Lenskart) |
| Comparison | Two versions side by side | "[A] vs [B]" | Rani A/B quiz; CR "$500 vs $30" |
| Question | Plant the question directly | "Can you [surprising thing]?" | Rani quiz |
| Education | A step-by-step for one task | "Here's the easiest way to [task]" | Rani ₹2,000 test, ₹50,000 brand (20-36k) |
| List | An ordered set of items | "These [4] [places] [do surprising thing]" | **Rani 4 cities (2.6M)** |
| Contrarian | A bold take against the grain | "[Common belief] is wrong" | Rani "cheap shoes theory" (853k) |
| Personal experience | A first-person story | "When I [did X], I found [Y]" | Rani's lived lines |
| Problem | A pain the viewer already feels | "If you struggle with [X], this fixes it" | Rani repeat-clients, management reels (24-36k) |

C says: pick the 1-2 formats the niche's outliers already use. In Rani's data, list and contrarian hooks produced
the two biggest reels; education and problem hooks sat at her baseline.

## 2. The 4 hook commandments (C): run every hook through them
1. **Alignment.** The spoken hook, the text on screen and the visual say the same thing. (Matches R: title band =
   first words; CR: frame 0 carries the headline.)
2. **Speed to value.** Zero delay, zero fluff. Stop after one sentence: is it clear what comes next?
3. **Clarity.** After one sentence the viewer knows what the video is about. No words they'd have to look up.
4. **Curiosity.** It opens a question they want answered.

## 3. The house hook rules (all must pass)
| # | Rule | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| K1 | About 12 words or fewer, finished by about 3.5 s; first sentence 16 words max | CR reel-scripts (measured) |
| K2 | A famous name or a number in the first breath; number hooks beat plain claims (49.5K vs 5.7K median) | CR story-reel formula (measured), R |
| K3 | A famous name in the first 4 words | U, 20 Oct |
| K4 | The 2-second test: a stranger names the subject, the number and why it matters | CR first-three-seconds |
| K5 | Says what the video is (click confirmation) in plain words a 14-year-old knows | C, U 20 Oct |
| K6 | A gap: a price gap, a belief flip, a contrast or a secret | R, CR |

## 4. Callaway's intro framework, squeezed for short form
His long-form intro has 4 parts. In a 50-second reel each part becomes about one line:
1. **Context:** what the video is about (K5).
2. **Common belief:** what the viewer already thinks ("Everyone says AI will do your shopping").
3. **Contrarian turn:** what's actually true ("Here's what happened when 4 giants tried").
4. **Proof and plan:** why trust it and what's coming (a count, a source, the lived line).
Use 1 and 3 always; 2 when a common belief exists; 4 can be a count ("4 giants").
