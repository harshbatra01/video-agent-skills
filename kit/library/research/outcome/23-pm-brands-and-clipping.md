# 23 Prediction-market brands: what they post, and what their clipping programs pay for

Researched 14 Oct 2026 for the Outcome job. Track: the official social accounts of prediction-market and nearby betting brands, their creator and clipping programs, and what a faceless Outcome page should copy. Files 00, 02, 03 and 20 to 22 are not repeated here (03 already covers platform rules and the Polymarket and Kalshi clipping scandals).

## Read me first

1. **Where the numbers come from.** 12-month account totals and per-post views for Instagram and TikTok come from Socialpruf's public pages (window 13 Oct 2025 to 13 Oct 2026; Socialpruf calls views "impressions"). TikTok per-video numbers were re-read from TikTok's own counters through tikwm on 14 Oct and are exact. Instagram hides views from logged-out readers, so Instagram views are Socialpruf's. X numbers are likes from the public syndication timeline (13 to 14 Oct; X shows no views there).
2. **Ratio** = views divided by the account's followers today (Oct 2026). Older posts look a little better than they were, because the account was smaller then.
3. **"Ad"** = TikTok's own is_ad flag on the post (a paid boost or an ad-authorised post). Several big numbers are bought reach, so the like rate is given where it matters. Organic hits here run at 5 to 12 likes per 100 views; boosted ones can sit under 0.2.
4. **"Collab"** = an Instagram co-authored post. Socialpruf counts it for both accounts. "By" names the first author, read from the post's public page. This matters: many of the brands' best posts were made by partner pages and creators, not by the brand.
5. **Seen, not guessed.** 36 examples were downloaded (TikTok and Instagram videos, carousel slides, two Outcome X videos) and turned into timestamped contact sheets in `raw\23\sheets\` (index at the end). Every description marked (seen) comes from those sheets.
6. Anything I could not confirm is marked UNVERIFIED. No number here is estimated.

**What flops (same study):**
- Bare odds or question cards. FanDuel Predicts' TikTok (13.38K followers) posted World Cup question cards ("Will Lionel Messi become the leading scorer in World Cup history?") that got 377 to 902 views each in June 2026 (0.03x to 0.07x), then went quiet after 23 Jun. This is the format the client called boring.
- News plus odds on autopilot. Kalshi's TikTok (690K) posts news carousels with a market card on the last slide about 3 times a day, at times that cluster on :29 and :59 past the hour (a scheduler, my inference). It averaged 11.5K views a post over 12 months (0.017x), the weakest ratio in the study (Hantavirus carousel, seen: 144,263 views, 0.21x).
- Link posts. "New Market: ..." posts on X from Myriad (83K followers) get 0 to 12 likes; PredictIt (40K) 0 to 8; Polymarket's own "🚨 NEW POLYMARKET" links 35 to 225 while its "JUST IN" news posts reach 1,462.
- Horizontal brand trailers. Outcome's own launch video on X (16:9, kinetic type, seen) got 2,864 likes; its Worlds 2026 trailer 79.

**What an Outcome page should copy, in order:** (1) the odds carousel (#1) as the signature series, posted the day a story moves a price; (2) before/after and meme carousels (#2, #4) and plain football moments (#7) as the volume that builds the audience, 5 to 10 posts a day; (3) "TRENDING" news clips with the market in the caption (#3); (4) match-moment clips with live cards (#5) and anime or hype "Part 1" previews (#6) as tests; (5) co-authored posts between our pages and @outcomexyz (#8) for every hit; (6) on the agency's other football and meme pages, the white-frame clip with a small Outcome banner or market slip under it (#10), disclosed as an ad.

**What to avoid:** odds-only cards and "new market" link posts; slide numbers that disagree with the caption; "biggest payouts" and win-flex claims; fake insider news (a Kalshi-badged X account was stripped of its badge in Dec 2025 after viral fake reports, Front Office Sports 18 Dec 2025); edgy political or ethnic memes (Polymarket Sports' "Number of children: Philip Rivers 10, Minnesota Somali daycares 0" photo, 6.29M, 29 Dec 2025, seen, is the kind of post that ends partnerships); undisclosed paid posts (file 03).

## (d) The 10 clearest lessons

1. **Story first, odds second.** On football Instagram the odds carousel is the proven hit (10 of PolymarketFC's top 20 posts are odds lists, up to 73x its followers), but every one opens on a story, a famous face and a trophy ("19 year old Lamine Yamal is projected to win the Ballon d'Or"). Bare odds or question cards flop: FanDuel Predicts got 377 to 902 views a post on 13.4K followers; Kalshi's news-plus-odds TikTok averages 11.5K on 690K and got 2.6K to 4.3K a post this month.
2. **One face, one giant number, one money line per slide, the real app last.** "$200 → $1,538 ON POLYMARKET" turns a % into a stake you can feel; the last slide is a real market chart and one CTA line. Keep the number identical on slide, caption and chart (PolymarketFC's Yamal post showed 55%, 51% and 50.8%) and leave out "biggest payouts" claims.
3. **Most of the feed carries no odds.** None of Polymarket Sports' 20 most-viewed TikToks mentions a price, and the biggest football post found (PolymarketFC's Ronaldo before/after, 41.8M, 129x) has none. Memes, moments, comparisons and tributes build the audience; the odds posts spend it.
4. **Carousels carry numbers, short clips carry moments.** 16 of PolymarketFC's top 20 posts are carousels or photos; all 20 of Kalshi Sports' are videos. On TikTok the organic hits are 8 to 17 second clips with one caption box (495,358 and 503,626 views on a 7.5K-follower account, 9 and 10 Oct), while 77 to 161 second recaps get 21K to 95K.
5. **Volume, speed, one account per sport.** The sports winners post 6 to 12 times a day (Polymarket Sports 4.57K Instagram posts in 12 months, PolymarketFC 3.90K, DraftKings 4.21K) and Polymarket runs separate Sports, FC and Hoops accounts. TikTok is hit-or-nothing: Polymarket's routine October clips got 1,088 to 1,904 views, one framed as a joke got 421,801.
6. **Reach is borrowed through collabs.** Kalshi's top Instagram posts are Messi's, Chalamet's and Giannis's own posts with Kalshi as co-author; 19 of Kalshi Sports' top 20 were made by creators (@joebonham, @offtheglasstv, @sports_multiverse_nba); Novig's biggest are Sydney Sweeney's; PolymarketFC co-authors with football pages and Fabrizio Romano. Our pages and @outcomexyz should co-author every post so the brand profile collects the views.
7. **The brand is small and physical, never a pitch.** A corner logo, a branded mic, a robot's jersey, an LED board, a "TRENDING" card, or a banner under the clip; the market goes in the last caption line ("Traders predict a 15% chance..."). None of the organic hits seen has a spoken sales pitch, and the 16:9 brand trailers (Outcome's own included) do worst.
8. **Clipping money buys volume cheaply, mostly through logos.** Polymarket's five known campaigns spent $266,174.99 for 591.9M views (about $0.45 per 1,000); its logo campaign paid $0.10 per 1,000 and bought 173.2M views for $15,000, and its best clip (12.1M) was a tweet-style meme with a "Get $20, code CLIP20" banner under it. Marketplace medians: $1.00 per 1,000 overall, $0.20 to $0.25 for logo work, $2.00 for UGC; podcast clipping paid $1.10 to $1.50.
9. **Briefs reward dedicated, geo-targeted, rule-following pages.** Polymarket demanded pages "fully dedicated to Polymarket", fresh accounts ("you cannot rebrand as polymarket"), 40 to 50% US or Tier 1 views, audience proof and 30-day bot checks; rejections hit reposts, off-topic clips, videos under 10 to 12 seconds, "slop", clips of kids and logos covered by app buttons. Per-clip caps ($200 to $1,000) and a 7-day earning window favour many steady clips over one viral hit. For Outcome, any brief must target countries where Outcome is allowed, not the US or UK (00-status, file 01).
10. **Discount paid reach, and keep it clean.** Several headline "hits" are ads (Kalshi Sports' 23.5M tailgate clip at 0.17 likes per 100 views; the 2.49M "fantasy hack" with 789 likes). The scandals are about hidden ads, fake wins and fake insiders (creators paid $2,000 to $3,000 a month and told not to disclose; a Kalshi-badged account spreading fake sports news). Disclose every paid post (#Ad near the start plus the platform toggle, as Content Rewards' own terms require) and never fake a win, a market or a source.
