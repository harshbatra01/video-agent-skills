## ARTIFACT 1: "Drill Edit Handbook"
URL: https://claude.ai/artifact/GoL6m1UuDdMH7ejwNZ1eQE

What it is: A Sept 2026 reference on how UK drill music videos are shot and cut (EsDeeKid, Central Cee, Digga D, the GRM Daily / Pressplay house look), organised around the real names editors use for each effect so that searches land.
It holds: 4 start-here links, EsDeeKid "4 Raws" credits, a 16-effect glossary, 41 tutorials by software, packs/presets/overlays, the run-and-gun kit, an 8-director table, search strings and channels.

### 1.1 Rules of thumb
- Three links plus one paid pack = about 90 percent of the toolkit (1.2). Everything else is depth.
- Half the obvious search terms are NOT what editors say, so you get no results and think the technique is secret. Use the real names in 1.3 (failed terms are listed per effect).
- The tutorial economy is built on New York drill, not UK drill: same toolkit, about 10x the results. Search strings: "new york drill music video effects" (not "uk drill editing"); "rap music video effects premiere pro"; "how to edit like jerry production" (Lil Durk's editor, most copied style in US drill); "how to edit like cole bennett lyrical lemonade"; "drill music video transitions free preset"; "music video beat shake premiere pro".
- Drill grade = crushed blacks plus ONE heavy cast, usually teal/orange or a single neon. [cc-grade, contrast-crush]
- RGB split is the signature look of the whole genre: one accent colour each side. [rgb-split, chromatic-aberration]
- Cutting to solid black or white on the beat is a drill signature (blank video cuts, photo bursts, strobe). [white-flash, strobe]
- Synced gunshot / reload / siren hits on the beat are as much the signature as any visual effect. Visuals without them read as a trap video, not drill. [NEW: sfx-sync]
- Fisheye is usually NOT a lens: it is Premiere's Lens Distortion applied in post. [lens-distortion]
- Best VHS look = a real second camera (camcorder) running live beside the A cam, real degraded tape against clean footage. Beats any VHS overlay. [vhs]
- Glow/halation on highlights can be done on the lens at capture (Moment Cine Bloom), hard to fake in post. [glow]
- Two-operator credit pattern: shooter/VFX + editor/colourist, both operate cameras, which is why the videos feel cut rather than assembled.
- "Run and gun" names the SHOOTING style, not an editing style; nobody calls the edit that.
- Style poles named in the director table: hyperactive, bright cutting (Suave) and slick cuts / transitions / camera tricks (Yukki) vs cinematic stillness, "the opposite pole to the frantic cut" (Dir.Lx) vs the classic look lifted only by selective FX and lighting (Ade OG).
- Sourcing: vendor-direct links only. Searching for packs surfaces cracked-warez mirrors (freevideoeffect, intro-hd, desirefx, gfxinspire, aaeplugins), commonly malware-laden. Avoid.

URL: https://claude.ai/artifact/34DKGT8tqFuZtmPbUd6c97

What it is: A Sept 2026 companion to the Drill Handbook for basketball and combat-sport edits: where the footage actually lives (the "logoless" scene-pack scene, official UFC / boxing channels, archive.org), a beat-by-beat build recipe per sport (14-18 s hoops, 12-16 s fight), and benchmark edits and accounts with exact view counts.
It holds: logoless sources, basketball build + audio + inspo, fight sources, "aura cold open" quote bank, fight build + inspo, rules shared by both sports.

### 2.1 Rules of thumb (all sport edits)
- Upscale BEFORE you crop: Topaz 1080p to 4K first, then crop to 9:16, then grade. Cropping first throws away resolution you cannot get back. Nearly every logoless channel does this and says so in the title.
- Cut on the bar, not on the action. The clip should already be in motion when you cut to it; starting a clip from the beginning of a play is the most common amateur tell. [beat-cut]
- "No watermark" means the pack maker's logo is gone, not that the footage is licensed. Every source here is broadcaster copyright.
- Top NBA edits: 18 to 65 seconds, 4K, ONE player, one track, one idea. Not a mix, not a career retrospective.
- Speed ramp into the release or wind-up, real time on contact. Reverse it and every shot or punch looks weightless. [speed-ramp]
- Flash only where it earns it: the beat-switch moment (hoops) and the connecting frame (fight, 2-3 frames). [white-flash]
- Hold the last frame a full beat past the music (hoops), or cut the music a beat early and let room tone run (fight). The hold / silence is what makes people rewatch. [freeze-frame]
- Open quiet and let the beat be the volume (a shouted opener has nowhere to go). Aura reads as controlled, not aggressive.
- Basketball is NOT the football phonk formula: Travis Scott, Kanye, jersey club, Detroit / Memphis rap (2.3).

### 2.2 Basketball build (target 14-18 s, 6-8 clips, cut on the bar)
- 0:00-0:02 Cold open (before the beat): slow push on a face, or a held still. No music yet, or crowd ambience only. This is the retention window, so the subject must be recognisable in frame one. [slow-intro]
- 0:02 First hit (the drop): hard cut on the downbeat into the first play, already in motion. Never start a clip from the dribble up; start one beat before the move. [build-to-drop, beat-cut]
- 0:02-0:08 Three plays, one per bar: escalate handle, then finish, then something absurd. Speed ramp into the release, real time on contact: "the single move that separates a good basketball edit from a highlight reel". [speed-ramp]
- 0:08-0:09 Beat switch (the reset): black frame, white flash, or a freeze on the release. Kill the audio for two frames. Change era, player or colour grade here. [beat-switch, white-flash, freeze-frame]
- 0:09-0:15 Second half, faster: clips roughly half the length of the first section. RGB split on each make. Text lands here if used. [beat-cut, rgb-split]
- 0:15-0:18 The button (payoff): biggest play last, freeze on the reaction, hold a full beat past the music. [freeze-frame]

### 2.3 Basketball reframe, audio, subjects
- Reframe: the main NBA camera is a wide side-court angle. A static 16:9 to 9:16 crop often loses the ball handler entirely. Keyframe the crop position and follow the player; two or three keyframes per clip is usually enough. Football tolerates a static crop (tighter iso cams), basketball does not. (Artifact says a 9:16 crop throws away about 44 per cent of the width; see caveat 1.)
- Score bug reason: broadcasters moved the NBA score bug to bottom centre ON PURPOSE so it survives a 9:16 crop (documented). A centre crop guarantees keeping the bug; football's top-corner bugs crop away cleanly. That is why the logoless pack scene exists: source from it instead of cropping broadcast yourself.
- Audio: Travis Scott, Kanye, jersey club, Detroit / Memphis rap. Phonk is comparatively rare in NBA edits. Songs confirmed from real high-performing edits: "Just Wanna Rock" (Lil Uzi Vert) on a Ja Morant edit (2.33M); "Runaway" (Kanye) on Curry (947k); "SDP Interlude" (Travis Scott) on Jordan (609k); "FE!N" on Kyrie (393k); "Highest in the Room" on LeBron (245k).
- Pattern: "SDP Interlude", "FE!N", "Runaway", "Highest in the Room", "Murder On My Mind" = Travis Scott and Carti almost entirely; the whole top tier runs on maybe seven songs.
- Subjects in rotation (per artifact, Sept 2026): Jalen Brunson, 2026 Finals MVP (32.6 per game; 45 in the Game 5 clincher; Knicks' first title since 1973); the Game 4 comeback (largest in Finals history, 29 points erased, OG Anunoby tip-in with 1.2 s left, 107 to 106; "the single most edited clip of the year"); Wembanyama (32 in Game 3, Spurs' only win, "alien" framing).

### 2.6 Fight build (target 12-16 s; the cold open does more work here than in any other vertical)
- 0:00-0:03 The line, over black or a still: 2-4 s of the quote. Voice starts BEFORE any visual. Grade the clip heavily or put it over black entirely so the face reveal has somewhere to go. [hook-first-second, slow-intro]
- 0:03 Hard cut (the drop): cut on the beat straight into the walkout or first exchange. No transition; the absence of an effect is the effect. [build-to-drop, beat-cut]
- 0:03-0:07 Walkout and face-off (build): slower clips, 1-1.5 s each; weigh-in and presser footage earns its place here. Buy tension before you spend it.
- 0:07-0:11 Exchanges (accelerate): clips get shorter. Slow motion on the wind-up, real time on contact. [speed-ramp, beat-cut]
- 0:11-0:12 The finish (impact frame): white flash on the connecting frame, 2-3 frames only, then hold the drop a full beat. "The one place a flash is not lazy." [white-flash]
- 0:12-0:16 Aftermath (the exhale): freeze on the reaction, corner or crowd. Cut the music a beat early, let room tone run; the silence is what people replay. [freeze-frame]
- One rule: aura = controlled, not aggressive. Do not open on shouting; open quiet, let the beat be the volume. Quiet reads as aura, loud belongs on the drop.

## Caveats and gaps (my notes, not from the artifacts)
1. Number check: the artifact says a 9:16 crop of 16:9 "throws away about 44 per cent of the width". For a full-height crop the loss is about 68 per cent (keeps about 32 per cent). Treat the 44 figure as unreliable; the keyframe-and-follow advice still holds.
2. Nothing here was re-verified today: links, view counts, 2026 NBA facts, quote provenance and "dead link" statuses are as compiled in Sept 2026.
3. Licensing: the artifacts themselves say every source is broadcaster copyright and "no watermark" is not a licence; Ballislife Content ID claims; ufc-ripper pulls paywalled Fight Pass content. Weigh this before using any of it on client posts.
4. Coverage gaps vs our brief: no football; nothing on roto cut-outs / text-behind-subject / 3D / particles / song cards beyond pointers; the two beat sheets (flash + speed ramp + freeze + RGB split) sit near the current Mbappe "GASS" bar rather than above it. The drill artifact is music-video, not sports, so its value is effect vocabulary, preset packs and the SFX-sync rule.
5. Highest-ceiling leads inside the artifacts: Itai Rosen (heavy VFX NBA), God Mode FX and Navi Prod. FX reels (https://youtu.be/LgoB98Ajgkk , https://youtu.be/bfsHljwtuoM), IGN EDITS sequences (e.g. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MVykqpvhBRU), Hizzer Edits / Mike Ciavarro / Owl Prod. for structure, P3DITS for a repeatable short format.
