## A. Premium athlete and founder supplement ads

**Examples**
1. **AG1 "Good Morning, Moon"** (Nov 2025, 60s). Athletes at dawn, with Rick Rubin reading a reworked "Goodnight Moon" over a score written for the ad. It is almost all B-roll under one calm voice, and the product is shown as a daily habit rather than as the hero. [iSpot](https://www.ispot.tv/ad/BFnE/ag1-good-morning-moon-feat-sloane-stephens-mick-fanning-eliana-rodriguez-cam-smith) · [Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/good-morning-moon)
2. **AG1 x Hugh Jackman** (Jan 2026, 30s). The star talks plainly about his own morning routine, with the product in his hand early. [iSpot](https://www.ispot.tv/ad/gZr6/ag1-simple-and-trusted-way-of-getting-nutrition-featuring-hugh-jackman). Their [ASMR spot](https://www.ispot.tv/ad/qnjG/athletic-greens-ag1-asmr-made-for-everybody) shows scoop, pour and stir sounds carrying the product shots.
3. **Symprove x Susannah Constantine** (UK, Jul 2025). A 60-something presenter and a UCL pharmacy professor explain the science while racing down a water slide. One visual idea makes a dry point fun for an older UK audience. [Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/serious-science-in-every-shot) · [paid social breakdown](https://motionapp.com/library/symprove)
4. **Bulk "Enhance Performance, Legally"** (UK). Opens straight to camera with "Why do I take DOPE?", then an intense training montage and a twist reveal. [Ads of the World](https://www.adsoftheworld.com/campaigns/enhance-performance-legally)
5. **Grüns**. User-made clips plus 60-something celebrity Lisa Rinna. Close-up, high-contrast product shots styled like beauty ads, with benefits shown as on-screen text. [Motion](https://motionapp.com/library/gruns)
6. **Warning: Huel.** A podcast-style ad with founder Julian Hearn and Spencer Matthews was banned by the ASA on 30 Sep 2026, because its story tied an athlete's race win to Huel. A Paula "illness to winning" ad carries the same risk. [City AM](https://www.cityam.com/huel-rapped-for-irresponsible-spencer-matthews-ad/)

To look further: [Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) (set country to UK and search the brands), and TikTok Creative Center > Top Ads (industry: Health).

**Patterns to copy**
- **Hook (0-2s):** open mid-action on the strongest image with a line that sets the stakes. Never open on the logo. TikTok says to land the key message in the first 3 seconds and recommends 21-34s ads ([TikTok](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/creative-best-practices?lang=en)). Example: "Paula has taken this every day since 1998" over archive footage.
- **Shot length:** about 1.5-2.5s on average for 40-65 viewers, with faster 0.5-1s bursts in montages. Hold product, claim and end-card shots for 2-3s.
- **B-roll:** anthem films are nearly all B-roll under a voiceover. Ads led by a person keep that face on screen and cut away for proof, so your ~40% rule suits a Paula-led ad.
- **Captions:**
  - Show 2-4 words at a time, large, white on a dark plate, with the keyword in brand gold.
  - On Reels, keep text clear of the top ~14% and bottom ~35% ([safe zones](https://adsuploader.com/blog/meta-ads-safe-zones)).
  - Allow about 1 second per 3 words of reading time.
- **Transitions:** hard cuts on the beat, match cuts (scoop to running stride), and a few whip pans or speed ramps with whooshes. Skip glitch, RGB-split and flash effects.
- **Product:** show it in use by 3-5s (scoop, pour, sip, with real sounds). End on a held shot of the pouch with the offer.
- **Sound:** Meta found Reels ads with both music and voiceover scored +15 points on positive response compared with ads without sound ([Meta](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/reels-ads-updates-performance-features-automated-creative-suitability-solutions)).

The same edit runs on both Meta and TikTok, so use one library that covers both. Epidemic Pro/Business or Artlist Pro is simplest. If a freelancer does the editing, make sure the plan covers client work.

**Why trending songs are out:** the music libraries inside the apps are licensed for personal use only. Meta bans music used for commercial purposes without your own licences ([guidelines](https://www.facebook.com/legal/music_guidelines)), and TikTok Business accounts only see the Commercial Music Library. Putting a chart song in an ad needs a sync licence from the publisher and a master licence from the label, often costing thousands. Without them, ads get muted, rejected or claimed.

**Search terms**
1. **Comeback story:** "solo piano / cello, reflective" for the illness section, then "pulsing strings, heartbeat, tension build" for the turn, then "hybrid orchestral, triumphant, trailer, taiko and brass" for the win.
2. **Confident premium:** "cinematic hip hop", "epic hip hop / orchestral trap", "modern orchestral pulse", "minimal luxury, driving". This gets the trending feel without the licensing risk.
3. **Light Q&A:** "warm lo-fi house", "chill pop instrumental", "soft funk groove". Avoid ukulele, whistling, glockenspiel and claps, which are the "goofy corporate" sound.

**Switching and building**
- Change tracks on a downbeat at the section break.
- Hide the join with a 1-2 bar riser that lands on a boom, or with half a beat of silence.
- With stems: piano and strings alone first, add drums at the turn, full mix at the win.
- To fit 15s or 30s exactly, use Epidemic's [Adapt](https://www.epidemicsound.com/tools/adapt/) (stems and length presets) or Premiere's [Remix](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere-pro/using/remix-audio-in-premiere-pro.html).

## C. Sound design

**Where each effect goes**
- **Whoosh:** whip pans and text slides.
- **Riser (2-4s):** into the turning point or the product reveal, ending exactly on the cut.
- **Boom/impact:** big numbers, title cards, the hero pouch and the logo. No more than 3-4 per ad.
- **Camera shutter:** archive photos popping on.
- **Paper rustle:** headlines, calendar flips.
- **UI pop/click:** captions, ticks, Q&A cards.
- **Typewriter:** typed dates and quotes, used sparingly.
- **Star ding or sparkle:** ratings and a logo glint.
- **Product sounds:** scoop, pour, stir.

**Mixing under voice**
- Duck the music 12-18 dB under speech using Essential Sound > Ducking ([Adobe](https://helpx.adobe.com/premiere/desktop/add-audio-effects/adjust-volume-and-levels/automatically-duck-audio.html)).
- Dip the music a few dB around 2-4 kHz. Older ears lose clarity there.
- Put hits in the gaps between words.
- Phone speakers can't play deep bass, so layer each boom with a 100-250 Hz thump.
- Aim the finished mix at about -14 LUFS with peaks no higher than -1 dBTP. This is common practice; neither Meta nor TikTok publishes an official target.

**Sources**
- **Free:** [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/sound-effects/) and [Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/free-sound-effects/) (ads allowed, no credit needed), and [Sonniss GDC bundles](https://gdc.sonniss.com/) (large libraries, royalty-free).
  - Avoid BBC Sound Effects: its licence is non-commercial.
  - Zapsplat's free tier requires a credit on screen.
- **Paid:** Epidemic Sound (its Pro plan covers ads) and Artlist Music & SFX Pro.

## D. AI product B-roll

**Tools (Oct 2026)**
- **Nano Banana Pro** for stills: takes up to 14 reference images, renders readable text, outputs 2K/4K ([Google](https://blog.google/technology/ai/nano-banana-pro/)). Nano Banana 2 is cheaper. API cost is about $0.05-0.15 per image for Nano Banana 2 and $0.13-0.24 for Pro ([pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Gemini app images on the Free and Pro tiers carry a visible watermark.
- **Gemini Omni Flash**, now Google's default video model: 9:16, up to 3 reference images, 3-10s clips, extendable to 40s, edit by chat ([docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/omni)). In the UK it will not let you upload or edit recognisable people.
- **Veo 3.1:** 9:16, 4/6/8s clips (1080p only at 8s), first-and-last-frame control. $0.40/s standard; the Fast version starts at $0.10/s ([docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)).
- **Flow monthly credits:** Google AI Plus 200, Pro 1,000 ([plans](https://gemini.google/subscriptions/)).
- **Runway:** Gen-4.5 costs 12 credits a second, so the $12/mo plan gives about 50 seconds a month. It also hosts Kling 3.0 and Seedance ([pricing](https://runway.com/pricing)).
- **Others:** [Kling 4.0 / 3.0 Omni](https://kling.ai/), [Hailuo MiniMax H3](https://hailuoai.video/), and [Higgsfield](https://higgsfield.ai/), which bundles Seedance, Kling, Veo, Sora 2 and Nano Banana Pro.
- **Budget example:** 6 shots × 4 tries × 8s costs about $19 on Veo Fast, or about $77 on standard.

**Keeping the pouch real**
1. Photograph the real pouch front-on, at 3/4 and in a hand, and use those photos as references.
2. Make the start and end stills in Nano Banana Pro.
3. Animate them with first-and-last-frame control. Keep the camera simple, and don't spin the pouch, because the text warps.
4. Track the real packshot over any frame where the logo is readable, or generate the scene empty and composite the pouch in.
5. Mute the AI-generated audio. Show hands only, no AI faces.

For a stylised look, try clay stop-motion, paper-cut collage, soft 3D or ink-line animation.

**Prompts (attach the pouch references, set 9:16)**
1. **Shelf clear-down**
   - Start still: "Premium product photo of a bright British kitchen shelf crammed with unbranded supplement bottles, soft morning window light, 50mm, shallow focus. The navy-and-gold pouch from the references stands centre; keep its logo and text exactly."
   - End still: "Same shelf, angle and light; only the pouch remains, with a glass of water."
   - Motion: "Locked-off camera; the bottles drop out of frame one by one in a quick stop-motion rhythm; the pouch never moves."
2. **Ritual macro:** "100mm macro: a scoop of powder [match the real colour] tips into a glass of water on a pale oak counter and swirls as it dissolves, slow motion, low sun, slow push-in, hands only."
3. **Paper-cut:** "Handmade paper-cut stop-motion: a kitchen calendar flips through the months while a paper sun rises and sets; a navy-and-gold pouch on the counter (swap in the real packshot later)."

**UK rules**
- The ad code applies to AI content like anything else: you are responsible even if a tool made it, and a realistic AI celebrity "endorsement" is "likely breaching the Code" ([ASA, Jun 2026](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/ai-and-deepfakes-four-things-advertisers-need-to-know-before-they-hit-run.html)). Never generate Paula.
- AI shots must match the real pack, size, powder and ingredients. No glows, "shields" or before/after effects. Use unbranded bottles.
- Supplement rules ([CAP 15](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/15.html)):
  - Only authorised health claims (15.1.1).
  - Nothing implying the product prevents or treats illness (15.6.2). This is the key risk for the comeback ad.
  - Nothing implying a normal diet isn't enough (15.8).
- **Paula co-owns Immufix** ([2026 purchase](https://www.lincsonline.co.uk/stamford/news/elite-athlete-paula-radcliffe-buys-lincolnshire-business-aft-9473896/)). The ASA says endorsements must disclose an investor's or director's stake ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/testimonials-and-endorsements.html)), so the ads should make her ownership clear.
