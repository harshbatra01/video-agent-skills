# Vertical paid ads with a presenter (supplement and wellness brands)

Worked case: 8 vertical paid-social ads for Meta and TikTok (1080x1920, 60 fps, 21 to 51 s, plus 5 s when a CTA end was
appended), for a daily nutrition powder fronted by a famous athlete who co-owns the brand (29 Sep to 4 Oct 2026).
Claims, advertising codes, archive and music licences: `research-and-sourcing`. Cutting, B-roll, grade and sound:
`editing-playbook`. Titles, captions and cards: `motion-design`.

## 1. The reviewer's script bar for testimonial ads
- Every ad needs a real CTA with an offer ("every single ad fundamentally needs like a call to action").
- Never trash other brands ("don't need to shit on people to make your thing looks good").
- Tell a story with emotion, not website copy ("it's no story").
- Use lines only the star can say ("you could get an ai to say this" was the verdict on a generic line).
- Hooks are specific direct address: "if you signed up for a marathon this year" beat a generic opener. Liked: an
  Instagram-style reply card, and "the three most important vitamins I used when I was a marathon runner".
- Keep a wellness product from looking like a sports product ("I don't want this product to seem too much like an
  athletic sports product").
- Why: these notes decided which scripts got shot, and they are the reviewer's standing taste for ads.

## 2. A star who co-owns the brand is proof of routine, not an endorsement
- Lead with preparation and consistency ("the race is one day, the routine is every day"); the product is part of her
  day, never the cause of a result. Why: the brief asked for "proof of philosophy and long-term routine, not simply
  celebrity endorsement", and it keeps clear of performance and cure claims.
- Ride the star's fame: "she has had A CAREER she is FAMOUS and yet we are not utilising on it" (reviewer). Archive of the
  star wherever it fits (asked for in every ad, in place of generic stock), headlines that really ran, camera-click photo
  montages, year counters ("TAKING IT SINCE [year]").
- Flag the risky story shapes even when the client parks compliance (detail: `research-and-sourcing`): "illness, then
  product, then recovery" is the highest-risk pattern (a UK regulator reportedly banned a rival brand's ad on 30 Sep 2026
  for tying an athlete's race win to the product); a co-owner's stake may need disclosing; never a realistic AI likeness
  of the star.

## 3. The premium ad pattern (research, 1 Oct 2026; not yet a verdict)
- Open mid-action on the strongest image with a stakes line, never on the logo; the key message inside 3 s.
- 21 to 34 s long (TikTok guidance). Our ads ran 21 to 51 s.
- The product in use by 3 to 5 s with real sounds (scoop, pour, sip).
- End on a held product shot with the offer.
- Average shot 1.5 to 2.5 s for a 40 to 65 audience; 0.5 to 1 s bursts in montages; the product, the claim and the end
  card held 2 to 3 s.
- Example hook: "[star] has taken this every day since [year]" over archive.
- Built from platform guidance and breakdowns of four premium supplement brands' ads.

## 4. What the reviewer's first review fixed (and the rules it left)
- "corporate asf", music "goofy corporate": fixed with more archive of the star, faster visual changes and music with
  stakes. Avoid ukulele, whistling, glockenspiel and claps ("goofy corporate"); "cinematic hip hop" and "modern orchestral
  pulse" read as confident premium.
- The scrub test: would I scrub or skip here? A talking head that runs 4 s or more with no visual change is a dead
  stretch to fill (method: `editing-playbook`).
- "trim the fat"; "show not tell": the B-roll shows the literal thing the line names ("her SMILING will not pass we need
  of her drinking specifically", editor).
- The product hero shot lands and holds still before the cut ("move then cut" was the note); the product fully visible
  and centred for most of the shot.
- The name title in focus and centred ("middle chest") without clashing with captions. Final: a bold condensed name with a
  small brand-gold pill, below the caption line. A dark glass plate was "off-putting" and covered her mouth; a
  sports-broadcast strap was liked.
- In an "N things" ad, hide the list and reveal each item as it is said ("we wanna keep em hooked right so we cant tell
  everything in start").
- The editor's quality reference set the bar, not the style ("THAT IS NOT THE STYLE WE WANNA DO EXACTLY, its just to show
  you how high quality stuff looks").
- Motion design was signed off on 3 Oct ("all the motion design and everything is good now we just need everything to
  sound nice"), and the SFX pass on the same day ("ok sfx is perfect").

## 5. Do not imply what you cannot back
- Light one star, not five, in a "1 star reviews" ad: five implies a rating nobody can back up.
- Never present stock people as the star's family; no strangers' faces in stock for a celebrity ad (hands and objects
  are fine). The stock licence forbids implying endorsement.
- Headline cards only for headlines that really ran, word for word.
- Time-jump lines ("two weeks later", "three months later") get real footage of the event; when the exact event is not
  online, use the nearest fitting real one and say so in the report.
- Lines that expire: an age ("I'm [age]"), "this year", "the record stood N years" stop being true on a date. Note the
  date in the build notes.

## 6. Platform rules for paid vertical ads (as of Oct 2026)
- No platform UI without permission: Meta requires permission to show Instagram's interface in ads, so design your own
  question card ("Ask [star]": a white card, an avatar crop, the question typed on word by word as she reads it).
- Safe zones on 1080x1920: aim to keep text clear of the top ~14% and the bottom ~35% (research; about 270 and 670 px);
  the smaller pixel margin also quoted, about 220 px top and 420 px bottom, is the hard limit. A top-left title sits
  under the Reels and Stories header and 4:5 feed crops cut it: move it to about 250 px from the top for feeds. The
  approved layout put captions low (kicker baseline y 1370, main caption y 1590 over a bottom gradient from y 980),
  titles just under the caption line, nothing over the face. It was never checked in an ad preview: do that before
  launch.
- Captions for ads (research): 2 to 4 words at a time, the keyword in the brand colour, about 1 s per 3 words.
- Sound (research, for client talk): Meta feed ads should still work muted; captions added 12% view time; Reels ads with
  music + voice-over scored 15 points higher positive response; music or voice-over gave up to 13% more incremental
  conversions (Dec 2025). On TikTok, 88% call sound essential and 73% stop for ads with audio (2021); custom music gave
  +34% brand recognition and +78% aided recall against recognisable songs (TikTok and MediaScience, 2021), while Kantar
  found famous songs add distinctiveness but little overall.
- Music: one library licence must cover both Meta and TikTok; in-app libraries are platform-only; trending sounds are not
  cleared for ads; a freelancer's plan must cover client work (detail: `research-and-sourcing`). TikTok ads need a
  Business Account, music added in TikTok's editor and the licence box ticked; a known bug mutes audio after product
  links; ads that never appear on a profile (Ads Manager only) are a grey area: ask the music library.
- TikTok TopView: no explosions, screams, phone rings or alarms in the first 3 s.
- Delivery: no platform publishes an upload loudness target; deliver -14 to -16 LUFS, at most -1 dBTP, 48 kHz. One
  round's renders measured -13 to -15 LUFS with true peaks at +0.1 dBTP: offer a normalised version.

## 7. The CTA end
- Every ad ends on the offer. A reusable CTA end card, a 5 s appended end on ads that lack one, the code card timing and
  the brand sting: `ctas-and-posting.md` section 3.
