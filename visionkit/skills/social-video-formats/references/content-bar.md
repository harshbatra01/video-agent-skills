# The content bar: what is worth posting, and why

Every rule here comes from a reviewer or editor verdict, or from a measured study, between Sept and Oct 2026. A rule that
rests on one remark and was never tested says "(tentative)". Verdict quotes are kept short and in the speaker's words.

## 1. The bar in the reviewer's words
- Content must be genuinely interesting, judged by **retention, CTR and AVD**, "not because the system is cool or
  impressive" (reviewer, 8 Oct 2026, reviewing a news batch).
  - Why: a new page has no loyal audience. Reach comes only from the story and the first seconds, so a clever pipeline
    that makes dull posts earns nothing, and weak or stale posts teach the algorithm the page is boring.
- New pages are "time and topic based, need to hit right": post what matters right now, and make it polarising or very
  current.
- Opinion versus posts ("A or B?") with no live story behind them are "empty" for a page with no audience; "this or that
  only works when you're bigger". Run them as Story polls instead (section 6).
- Stale stories fail: a story about a years-old TV show was "too old".
- Numbers always in a different colour, carousels included (section 5).
- The editor's bar for edits: "good wont cut we need GREAT"; "Anything flashy is good. Anything crazy is good. Anything
  basic is bad." (music edits, 13 to 14 Oct).
- What CTR, AVD and retention mean on each platform, and how to read them: `measuring-performance.md`.

## 2. The interest test (run it before building anything)
Score each candidate 0 to 2 on five things. Build only at 7 of 10 or more.

| Test | 2 points when |
|---|---|
| Fresh | it broke today or is still moving |
| Tension | two sides, a clock, a reversal, a surprising number |
| Audience fit | it matters to this page's niche (for a UK page, UK first) |
| First frame | frame 0 alone would stop a stranger (a face, a number, a conflict) |
| Added fact | we can add one fact the source does not show (section 3) |

- Cut posts that fail rather than fill a quota. Why: quality over quota; a weak post costs the page more than a missing
  one.
- Watch topic fatigue: "we've done too much [one artist] stuff" (editor, 12 Oct). Too much of one story lowers the page.
- Engagement posts need a real hook: a real number, a real poll result, a live beef.

## 3. Every post adds one fact the source does not show
- Add one fact a viewer could not get from the clip or the source post: a chart position, a time, a count, a date, a
  comparison, a change since yesterday, a reason, the backstory.
- The test: "could a viewer get this by reading the source?" If yes, add something or skip the post. Instagram's own test
  is "new information, context or a point of view not obvious from the original".
- Examples that worked: "1,700 comments in under 2 hours" (read off the video page); "23 years since [album] won the
  Mercury"; a price stamped "1 minute before kick-off".
- Does NOT count as original: a logo, a border, a watermark, subtitles, a credit line, a speed change, a screenshot of
  someone else's post, a headline that only repeats the clip. Credit alone never counts.
- Why: every big platform now cuts reach or money for reposted or templated work (as of Oct 2026, re-check; detail in
  `platforms-and-policies.md` section 4):
  - Instagram (30 Apr 2026): accounts that mostly post unoriginal reels, photos or carousels leave recommendations
    (followers still see them); recovery needs most posts original over a rolling 30 days; 75% of US recommendations now
    come from original posts.
  - TikTok (2026H2 guidelines, in force 24 Sep 2026): "unoriginal or reused material without anything new" is
    ineligible for For You, which drives about 7 in 10 views.
  - YouTube: a channel can lose monetisation for content that "looks like it's made with a template" or repeats with
    minimal variation.
  - Facebook (Mar 2026): reused footage counts only when the edit adds information, analysis or a better storyline.
  - A page at 0 followers has no other way to reach people: losing recommendations removes its only growth route.
- For templated pages: rotate the kind of post and its layout every 10 to 15 posts and vary hook wording and caption
  structure (vendor advice, anecdotal), but keep the page's one text treatment (section 8: new skins read as a template
  pack); never post the same file on two pages; give each page its own section of a shared video; keep working files
  for appeals.

## 4. Never fake a number, a poll, a quote, a trade or a DM
- The reviewer once suggested "25% think [A] is better" for a versus post. It was declined and replaced with real numbers.
- Honest stat hooks instead:
  1. Our own Story poll, posted the next day with the count: "[X]% of [N] voters on our Story picked [A]" (say "our
     followers, not a survey"; poll Tuesday, result Friday).
  2. Real public numbers side by side with the date. A carousel on two rival diss tracks used: 2.3M views in 10 days vs
     447K in 3; likes 78K vs 17K; comments 6.5K vs 2.5K; about 234K vs 149K views a day.
  3. Real quotes from the scene, word for word.
  4. Our own record: "We called [X] of our last [N] chart moves."
  5. Taste labelled as ours: "Our take: A. The numbers say B."
- Why: one exposed fake stat ends a news page's credibility. The platform will probably not catch a fake poll;
  screenshots will, and the cost is far bigger than the comments it earns.
- Make the honest version of a viral lie, never the lie:
  - The biggest football hook of Oct 2026 ("who wins the Ballon d'Or") was mostly fake: one fake "[player] won" post did
    14.8M views on 21K followers. The honest version: "one name is at 63%", with a date.
  - A WSJ study (Jun 2026) found 778 of 1,105 prediction-market TikToks showed trades apparently filmed on copies of the
    app, and a fake "53% odds" screenshot of a market that never existed went viral (Jan 2026). The fakes are now a news
    story and a legal risk.
  - A client brief format that faked a DM from a top trader with an AI-written reply was skipped.
- Every number on screen is checked and dated; numbers on frame 0 are final, never mid-count; on-screen facts in edits
  (a birth date, a record, a draft pick) are double-checked before posting. How to check: `research-and-sourcing`.
- AI "answers" shown on screen must be real model replies built on real data.
- Losses as honest as wins; real settled trades only; realistic money amounts.
- A game format with a draw (a wheel, a ball race) is one weighted draw with a logged seed (the date by default), and the
  post says it is a draw. Why: the result is reproducible and not staged.
- Never post leaks, unreleased snippets or unverified gossip; never present a stand-in as the real event (label it, and
  say so in the report).

## 5. Numbers: coloured, and the punchline, not the product
- Numbers take the accent colour automatically on cards, video headlines and every carousel slide (reviewer: the "#68" in a
  chart headline "in red"; editor: "carousels didnt haave colour emphasis"). Tweet cards stay plain like real tweets.
  Names that start with digits stay uncoloured (21 Savage, 50 Cent). Why: the number is usually the hook, and colour
  makes it read first in a scroll.
- The number is the punchline or the stakes, never the product. Evidence (14 Oct, 32 X timelines, 19,843 posts):
  - a bookmaker's own price posts got 4 to 13 likes; its banter with a number as the punchline got 2,911 to 23,133;
  - a big stake on a favourite that then won got 92 to 278 likes;
  - a predictions brand's question-and-odds cards got 377 to 902 views on 13.4K followers;
  - our own first card batch was the product kind: "These are not good posts. It's not worth posting them." (editor)
- Most posts carry no number at all: moments and memes build the audience, number posts spend it
  (`faceless-posts.md` section 3).
- One number per frame, never the same figure twice in one frame, and the same number on every slide of a carousel.

## 6. Polarising without bait
- Frame it: two named sides, one real fact, a clock, an open question ("Who's winning now?").
- Why: us-vs-them framing is the strongest share driver found: in 2.7M news and political posts, each out-group word
  raised the odds of a share by about 67% (PNAS 2021). That study is political; applying it to rap beef is an inference.
- Ask, never order: open questions are fine; orders to comment, share, tag, like or type a set word, number or emoji are
  demoted. Wording and examples: `ctas-and-posting.md` section 1.
- Versus and this-or-that posts live in Stories as polls until the page has an audience; the poll's real result can
  become the next day's post.
- No live court case or "allegedly" claim without a reliable outlet or police source behind it.
- Run the angle the audience already runs with: when a rapper's promised diss turned out to be a troll song, the top
  comments said "we got trolled" (700+ likes), so that angle led the post. Why: shares follow the joke people are
  already making. Two posts on two pages can tell one story (the live clip on one page, a pinned-comment card on the
  other).

## 7. What we skip on news and culture pages
- Deaths and tragedies; violent and prison-violence clips; thirst-trap clips (Instagram limits recommendations of
  suggestive posts); relationship gossip; edited or misleading images; race angles that can backfire; someone else's skit
  or meme edit; promos dressed as stories; unconfirmed collabs; leaks, unreleased snippets, agency paparazzi photos;
  too much of one story or one artist.
- Why: they lose reach, mislead, or put the page at risk.
- Weak by the data: throwbacks and plain release posts (0 of 6 throwbacks broke out in one week's scan); a low-pull
  freestyle; a nice story with no tension; recaps more than once a week.

## 8. Variety means different kinds of posts, not different skins
- "these are all SAME types of posts bro" (editor, 13 Oct) was said of 18 posts in one family of designed cards.
  Answering "we want variation" with a look kit (weather, broadcast, tabloid, paper, terminal and neon skins) read as a
  motion-template pack and was rejected too.
- Vary the format, the footage and the moment; keep one text treatment per page. Why: a new skin per format is itself an
  ad tell (`faceless-posts.md` section 9), and one consistent look makes a page recognisable from one frame.
- An earlier note to "rotate looks" (13 Oct) is superseded by this rule (14 Oct).
- Native beats designed: data cards and motion-graphic data videos were rejected ("the UI is bad", "It's not worth
  posting them", "I can't watch them", 13 Oct). Start from real moments and borrowed footage with one native caption.
- Hooks beat craft for reach: the most inventive motion-graphic edits often have under 3K views while hook-led Shorts get
  2M to 28M. Put the effort into the first second (`first-three-seconds.md`).
- Topic pull can outweigh craft: on one narrated mini-doc page, sports reels had a median 113K views against 5K to 9K
  for tech, finance, entertainment, consumer and luxury; finance and VC topics never passed 16K (`reel-scripts`).

## 9. Who decides taste, and how Claude behaves on a page
- Research defaults are not verdicts. Research said TikTok hits use a white rounded caption box with black lowercase
  text; the editor called it "TOOO off beat" and the house look won. Research said skip glitch, RGB-split and flash
  effects for a 40 to 65 premium audience; the editor then loved RGB-split dynamic captions. Show one mock frame of a new
  text style before building around it.
- Act as the pages' manager, not a builder (editor, 8 Oct: "actually working as a manager for the pages trying to grow
  them from start"; 9 Oct: "ideate, you're a good social media manager"): on a viral moment propose extras (an alternate
  opening for an A/B test, a timeline carousel, a Story poll, a pinned comment), pass on weak stories, and back every
  choice with research or numbers.
- Say no to a bad idea and give the honest version in the same reply (the fake stat in section 4). Why: the page's
  credibility and the team's trust outlast one post.
- Formats with no proof go in a "Tests" section, never the opening slot of a batch or a review page. Why: a weather-report
  skin with no proof flopped everywhere it was measured (4.8K views on 172K followers, 902 on 1.08M, 1 view).
- After a rejection, research real examples and show a plan with proof frames before building again; do not polish the
  rejected version (`faceless-posts.md` section 1).
