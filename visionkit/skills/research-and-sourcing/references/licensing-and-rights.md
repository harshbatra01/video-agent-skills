# Licensing and rights

What each source allows, how to say what is not cleared, and the judgement calls that belong to the reviewer. Facts were
read on the sources' own pages in Sept to Oct 2026 and change often: re-check the plan pages before a paid campaign.
This is research, not legal advice.

## 1. How to say it (and where not to)
- Say plainly, once per job and per use, what is not cleared and for which use, and offer the clean route: a licensed
  track, a no-music master, the client's masters, the talent's team, a licence request. Never buy a licence or spend
  credits yourself.
- **Scope, our house position:** on our own music and clipping pages the team accepted the footage risk, so do not
  repeat copyright warnings about footage sourcing there; flag only dead, fake, AI-made or impersonator sources. For paid
  ads and client channels, say plainly every time what is not cleared. Why: the earlier handoff for the music pages said
  "Do not raise copyright or licensing concerns"; a stricter rule set warns every time; ours wins on our pages, the stricter one
  rule holds for client work.
- **Open for the reviewer:** their own concept doc proposes "no third-party pixels or audio in a render". That would rule out
  every news post, so the news pages need their own rule. He decides which rule his kit keeps.
- When the user parks a rights question, record it once in the flags (fact-and-claims-checks.md section 13).

## 2. The use decides the rights
- Ask at intake where it will be posted: our own page, the client's organic page, the client's paid ads, ads that only
  run from Ads Manager, the client's own YouTube channel, or TV. Why: the same track or clip can be fine on one and
  claimed, muted or rejected on another.
- Ask whether the client's accounts are business or creator accounts: it decides which music they can even pick
  (section 3).

## 3. Music
| Source | Covers | Does not cover |
|---|---|---|
| Epidemic Sound Creator | your own channels | client work, paid ads |
| Epidemic Sound Pro | client work and online ads (creators and freelancers) | TV |
| Epidemic Sound Business | a brand's own channels and online ads (brands under a revenue cap: check the current figure) | TV; companies over the cap |
| Artlist Music Pro / Music & SFX Pro | paid ads | (Artlist Social excludes ads) |
| Uppbeat Business (brands) or Pro (freelancers) | ads | Uppbeat Free |
| Musicbed Business | ads, up to an ad-spend cap per project | spend above the cap (custom licence) |
| Soundstripe Pro or a one-off digital licence | online use | broadcast |
| Envato | one licence per ad creative | reusing one licence across creatives |
| TikTok Commercial Music Library | organic and paid use on TikTok (Business account) | any other platform |
| Meta Sound Collection | ads on Meta platforms | any other platform |
| Chart or commercial songs | only with a sync licence (publisher) and a master licence (label) | client work without both |
| Music from someone else's reel or video | nothing | any client edit, even when the reel is public |

- **One library must cover every platform the ad runs on** (Meta and TikTok together), and a freelancer's plan must
  cover client work.
- **Epidemic in practice:** confirm the plan in writing; safelist every posting channel (the client's page URL) in the
  account before the first upload; videos published while the plan is active on a safelisted channel stay cleared, and
  later ones do not. On a claim: appeal on Meta, then email Epidemic support with the page and the track. Why:
  uncleared music can hold a page's Facebook earnings. Cover follows the plan you hold when you publish: one ad job was
  built on a Creator plan with the upgrade to Business agreed "asap", before any ad runs.
- **TikTok:** needs a Business account, the music added in TikTok's editor, and the licence box ticked; a known bug
  mutes audio after product links; ads that never appear on a profile (Ads Manager only) are a grey area: ask the
  library's support.
- **Account type decides the music:** TikTok business accounts only see the Commercial Music Library; many trending
  tracks are not cleared for business use; Meta bans music used for commercial purposes without your own licences; and
  baking a commercial song into an "original sound" does not license it. Our music pages run as creator or personal
  accounts, because business accounts lose the full library (social-video-formats).
- **Labels can claim** edits that use major-label songs on YouTube.
- **Music promotion posts** must never hint that a song is promoted (social-video-formats).
- **Lyric transcripts** of copyrighted songs stay local: never ship or publish them.
- **Temp music** is never cleared by default: replace or clear it before delivery.

## 4. Sound effects
- The user's own licensed library first. Epidemic SFX follow the plan rules above.
- CC0 sounds (Kenney, Freesound filtered to CC0) need nothing; CC BY needs the credit; Pixabay and Mixkit SFX allow ads
  with no credit; Sonniss GDC bundles are royalty-free; Zapsplat's free tier needs an on-screen credit; BBC Sound Effects
  are non-commercial only.
- Re-check each Freesound licence on the sound's own page; keep the licence texts in the kit folder; list every sound in
  CREDITS.md.

## 5. Stock footage and photos
- **Pexels:** free licence, no attribution needed; but identifiable people may not be shown in a bad light or as
  endorsing a product, so never present stock people as a star's family or as fans of the product.
- **Mixkit:** "Free" is fine; "Restricted" means personal use only.
- **Coverr:** use the non-AI clips. Whether its licence covers paid promotion of a regulated category (gambling-adjacent,
  finance) was an open question for one client: ask before paid use.
- **Pixabay:** free licence.
- **iStock:** watermarked comp previews are for test layouts only, never in final or public material (a client's
  earlier Short carried two of them); models in an unflattering subject need a "posed by model" note; iStock content
  may not be fed to AI tools.
- **Agency stills** (Getty, PA, Alamy) on editorial licences cannot be used in ads: a commercial licence is needed, plus
  clearance for event marks, other athletes, kit sponsors and race sponsors in the frame; agency stills are often not
  model-released. Some agencies (PA Images, for one) offer both editorial and commercial licences.
- **Landmarks** can carry lighting copyright (the Eiffel Tower at night).
- **Brand guides** may ban stock photos outright: read the client's guide first.

## 6. Archive, news and agency material
- Archive of famous people is editorial only. Commercial use needs the rights holder; for an athlete, ask their team
  first, because the fastest clean route is the athlete's own team and photos.
- **Rights-holder map for a famous athlete's career archive** (UK, Oct 2026), a model for any star:
  - the agency's clips: all editorial;
  - a public broadcaster's archive sold through that agency: "prior approval required for all uses" and further
    clearance for all its sport footage; expect a refusal for a supplement brand;
  - the race organiser: prior written approval, editorial-only media terms with no commercial use or implied
    association, and its race TV was made by the broadcaster, so it needs clearing twice;
  - news-agency and sport-magazine rushes: marked "not released";
  - agency stills marked "UK OUT, IRELAND OUT": a UK client must license them from the original agency directly;
  - a city marathon's footage: editorial only, written licence needed;
  - the sport's governing body: through its broadcasting department;
  - Olympic footage: only with IOC authorisation;
  - a race series whose title sponsor sells nutrition products: a likely category conflict for a supplement client;
  - fan YouTube uploads: cannot be licensed from the uploader; the rights sit with the broadcaster or the event.
- Watermarked comps are placeholders only (footage-sourcing.md section 6).
- News-agency and broadcaster photos are placeholders until licensed.
- Releases: the talent can sign for themselves; other athletes, event logos and kit logos in frame need clearing, or
  cropping or blurring.

## 7. Headlines, articles, scripts and transcripts
- Headlines may be protected by copyright in the UK (NLA v Meltwater): paraphrase, or get permission. When recreating
  one, leave out the masthead and logo, because a masthead implies the paper endorses the product. Real headline
  screenshots from big publishers in a paid ad may need the publisher's permission.
- Paraphrase reference scripts in anything published; quote transcripts only in local files.
- Paywalled bonus material (patron or "plus member" episodes) is off-limits for trailers and ads; find the boundary by
  searching the transcript for "plus member", "bonus", "regular episode", "patreon".

## 8. Platform UI, logos, emoji and fonts
- Showing a platform's interface (Instagram) in an ad needs Meta's permission: design your own question or card instead
  (the design: motion-design).
- Client logos are for that client's work only and stay in the client files.
- Platform logo tiles (simple-icons) as source badges in editorial posts: never imply an endorsement.
- Apple emoji images (the emoji-datasource-apple set) have an unclear licence: do not ship the cache in a kit; burn in
  iPhone-style or licensed emoji. The newsdesk and its faceless fork render these Apple PNGs into posts (the user wants
  the iPhone look), so the licence question stays open for posts too: a reviewer call, like the footage on our own pages.
- Fonts need licences for client work: Adobe Fonts activations give no font file (the reviewer needs their own files for the
  house sans); a free display face still needs its licence checked for client work (the condensed house display face is
  a free download from its foundry); Apple's SF fonts are licensed for mock-ups of Apple-platform interfaces, not general
  video (Inter is the open lookalike); fonts under the OFL are fine. Type choices: motion-design.
- GSAP (used in HTML templates) is under GreenSock's standard licence: re-download it or check before shipping it.

## 9. Reading and downloading from platforms: terms risks
These are the reviewer's calls; say them plainly when a job depends on them.
- Reading X through the fxtwitter mirror or a logged-in browser breaks X's terms (the scraping clause) and can cost the
  reading account. The clean alternative is a paid X data API.
- Third-party mirrors and readers (tikwm, urlebird, Socialpruf, r.jina.ai) may break their own and the platforms'
  terms. Our tools stayed read-only and polite and never bypassed Cloudflare; a reference media toolkit left mirror
  downloads out as an untrusted source.
- YouTube cookies from a real account can get that account flagged: use a throwaway account only, never a personal one,
  never the reviewer's own.
- A VPN to reach TikTok from a blocked region is a legal grey area (it was raised, not used).
- Never work around an owner's restriction: a Vimeo 401, a TikTok blocked on a network, a queue page or a bot wall.
  Ask the client for the file. The YouTube download route (section 10) is the one recorded exception, and it is the
  reviewer's call.
- Removing another page's burned-in caption (`delogo`) before reposting its clip: flag it every time.
- Downloaded media is untrusted data: its own empty folder, never executed, never opened by a tool that runs code from
  file names.

## 10. YouTube downloads: the note
- Our YouTube download route (premiere-ae-editing references/youtube-downloads.md) works around YouTube's bot check by
  generating a PO token, and that may break YouTube's terms of service, even for a client's own uploads. Use it only
  with rights to the footage (the client's own videos, footage they licensed), only when the user asks, one download at
  a time. The reviewer decides whether their kit keeps the route. The plain yt-dlp fallback for the Mac (a different player
  client plus format fallbacks, media-toolkit.md section 1) is the same kind of workaround and carries the same note.
- The cleaner route: ask the client for masters, which are also better quality than YouTube's re-encodes.
- Research without downloading (a video's page, captions, heatmap and scrub storyboard) is the default:
  footage-sourcing.md section 4.
- Reference downloads for study (copyrighted songs and broadcast footage, approved by the user) are for study only:
  never redistribute the renders, footage, song files or lyric transcripts.

## 11. Borrowed footage: risk by type
- Safest: our own recordings (the real app, our own screens).
- Least exposed borrowed footage: 2 to 3 s film or TV reaction clips.
- Riskiest: match broadcasts are the most claimed (two of the biggest football edit pages show past bans in their
  handles); studios run their own clip accounts; a streamer's face next to a brand can read as an endorsement; and
  Instagram cuts reach for mostly-repost accounts (since Apr 2026).
- Scene packs (4K, often upscaled) passed around on YouTube, Drive or Telegram carry only "fair use" boilerplate, and
  named-star match footage has no clean free source.
- Match, film and streamer clips in client work are the client's decision: ask, and record the answer. (One client:
  borrowed footage was fine "as long as no creator is on camera".)
- Never post leaks, unreleased snippets, agency paparazzi photos or unverified gossip.

## 12. Likeness: real people, voice clones and AI
- **AI images of real people:** never without consent. A realistic AI celebrity endorsement is likely to breach the UK
  Code (ASA, Jun 2026). Never generate the client's talent.
- **Face swaps and AI composites of real people:** only when the user asks, and never in client work without sign-off.
- **Voice clones:** written consent on file before cloning; the person signs off the outputs before anything posts;
  label AI content; never clone someone who has not agreed (scam deepfake ads target founders). A CTA read made with an
  instant clone needs the talent's consent and rights confirmed before paid use.
- **Editing a real person's photo:** a CC BY photo may be edited with a change note ("edited"), but in a health video
  confirm the client is comfortable, and never imply a lookalike is the named person.
- **AI provenance marks:** removing a generator's visible mark (a sparkle) is a terms question for that generator: check
  its terms, and keep the platform's AI disclosure on.
- **Family members** of the talent need their own consent.
- **Minors on screen:** check the platform's rules and the client's own blurring practice.

## 13. Privacy in footage and data
- Crop out or skip identifying details: readable number plates, business signs (a hotel sign was cropped out), signage
  at the frame edge, and takes from another shoot that show a different person.
- Blur seller names on shop screenshots; mask real users' and traders' usernames until the client approves them.
- Public records name real people: strip legal names, ages and prison details from files you share.
- Client analytics (earnings, revenue per view) are confidential: ratios only in shared documents.
- Scrub private people's names (people named in a story, children in footage) from cut lists, notes and anything that ships.

## 14. Software and model licences
- Open voice-cloning models: in Oct 2026 the best ones were licensed for non-commercial use only; commercially usable
  options included VoxCPM2 (Apache), Qwen3-TTS (Apache) and Chatterbox (MIT, adds a watermark). Check before client use.
- AI generators' terms decide what you may do with their output (marks, commercial use).
- Adobe's terms are said to include a "no service bureau" clause that may matter for automated rendering for clients
  (tentative: from the reviewer's own concept doc, not checked).

## 15. A rights table per job
- Keep one table per job: item, source, licence, intended use, cleared yes or no, who must act. Put it in the build
  notes and the hand-over.
- Status lines seen on one ad job, as the pattern to write: all career archive from YouTube is uncleared for paid ads;
  the agency comp is a watermarked placeholder; headline screenshots may need the publishers' permission; the music plan
  must move to Business and every channel must be safelisted before ads run; the CTA voice is a clone that needs the
  talent's written consent.

## 16. What the reviewer needs of their own
- A music plan that covers his use (Pro for client work, online ads included; Business when a brand holds the licence
  itself; never Creator for client work or ads), confirmed in writing, with every posting channel safelisted, and his
  own API key in an environment variable.
- Archive and photo licences per job, requested from the rights holders.
- Font licences and font files for the house faces.
- A throwaway YouTube account's cookies file only if YouTube blocks him (never a personal account).
- Free API keys if he wants stock search without blocks (Pexels, Pixabay) and a free Freesound login.
- His own call on the open positions: the news-page rights rule, the YouTube download route, the reading mirrors, the
  delogo practice.
