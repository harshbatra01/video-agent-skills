# Editing playbook

The thinking behind our edits: what to ask first, how a job moves from brief to delivery, how stories, hooks, cuts,
B-roll, text, sound and colour are judged, and which choices reviewers approved or sent back, with their reasons and
their own words. Every rule came out of a real job between late September and mid October 2026 (premium vertical ads,
presenter-led YouTube explainers, cutdowns of long documentary footage, music and culture news pages, faceless
posts for a client, cracked sports music edits), plus a measured reference set from another studio.

Treat the rules as strong defaults. The user's own words, the client's brief and the user's hand edits in their latest
save win, and you say which default you dropped.

**Who is who:** "the user" is whoever runs this session. "The editor" and "the reviewer" gave most verdicts;
clients and their reviewers gave the rest. The second reviewer (another studio) is a reference for taste, not a client.

## The ten rules that cost the most when broken

1. **Confirm the format, the brief and the current assets before you build.** Aspect ratio, resolution and frame rate,
   length, deliverables, where it posts, what is approved, which asset folder is current. Why: a whole storyboard was
   built 9:16 because "reel" sounded vertical ("you should have asked me that"); older presenter stills were reused
   while newer ones sat in the shared folder, which cost 17 re-renders. Ask as numbered questions, each with a default.
   (references/job-flow.md)

2. **Build on the user's latest save, never destroy, always version.** Their hand edits are the brief: keep them and say
   what you kept. Never touch his original or another open project. Disable, never delete (captions, clips, B-roll he
   placed: "i will delete old one myself"). Any change to a delivered file is a new `_v2`, `_v3` ("do not replace this one
   make version 2. keep that in mind always"). Compile in Premiere as layers (music edits too); After Effects only for
   elements, never whole baked shots. (references/reviews-and-versions.md)

3. **Show it before you build it.** A plan, finished-looking frames, one mock of a new text style, 2 to 5 options with a
   recommendation, a short test of a new technique. Never build before the pick, and expect the pick to differ (Claude's
   recommended title was "so off putting"; option B won). Why: 40 designed videos built without proof frames were "not
   worth posting"; one frame of the white TikTok box would have caught "TOOO off beat". (references/job-flow.md)

4. **Story before effects.** Pass the logline test (one sentence a stranger understands), give the hook a promise
   without spoiling the payoff, keep the story in order, end on the payoff. Why: a short with no framed arc was "not
   coherent not a clear story bro"; a highlight-reel hook was "so underwhelming"; the hooks and story flow the editor
   loved followed premise, threat, action, twist. (references/storytelling-and-structure.md, references/hooks.md)

5. **Cut on the audio, never mid-word or mid-sentence, and cut tight where it counts.** Transcript times are 0.1 to 1 s
   off; every mid-sentence cut was rejected. Hooks and shorts: no pause over about 0.3 s, out about 0.2 s after the last
   word. Long cutdowns keep about 70 to 78% of the source, hooks 7 to 9 s. A shot that flashes for 5 frames is an error,
   not pace. (references/cutting-and-pacing.md)

6. **Show exactly what the line says, with real material.** The B-roll is the literal noun or action ("her SMILING will
   not pass we need of her drinking specifically"); real footage of the subject, real headlines and the real app screen
   beat stock, drawings and designed dashboards; no strangers' faces in a star's piece; keep the speaker's face on the
   hook, the points she presents and the offer. (references/broll-judgement.md)

7. **Text must read on a phone, stay off faces, and be true.** Small text gets a plate and size ("the white is clashing
   with the sky"); nothing over a face or mouth; captions step aside while graphics print the words (disable, never
   delete); on-screen words match the spoken words; numbers coloured and real; never a fabricated stat, poll, headline or
   rating. (references/captions-and-on-screen-text.md)

8. **The reviewer's taste wins, scoped to the spot.** A verdict beats research, Claude's recommendation and any default.
   A rejection applies to that placement unless the reviewer says "never" ("if u used it elsewhere and i didnt point it
   out there its fine there but here no"). "Don't fix, apply next" means change the defaults, not the delivered files.
   Check every new piece against the taste list before showing it. (references/taste-verdicts.md)

9. **Measure, never guess, and say exactly what you checked.** Claude cannot watch the playback or hear: frame sheets,
   loudness, sync reports, checks at every changed time in the real timeline, a listening note with five timecodes for
   the user's ears. Report warnings even after an approval. Feed every review back into the tool defaults as dated rules.
   (references/reviews-and-versions.md, references/working-with-claude.md)

10. **Respect the machine and the session.** Hold heavy renders until "go" when the user is running other things; one
    heavy job at a time; stop at once on wait, pause or stop; never work around a guard, a lock or another session;
    never type passwords or read keys; ask for the upgrade instead of quietly working around it.
    (references/working-with-claude.md)

## The job, start to finish
1. **Intake.** Read everything first (brief, client files and links, the example project's newest sequence, the
   voice-over, references, brand guide, earlier notes). Fact-check the client's own claims. Then ask only the user's
   calls (rule 1). New kind of job: inspect the real project, answer with facts and honest limits, offer a pilot.
2. **Study the material.** Transcripts and screenplays before frames; contact sheets; catalogue footage; find the
   logline, the arcs, the hook candidates (replay peaks), the payoff. Study any reference properly
   (references/studying-references.md).
3. **Plan and show.** A short plan; frames, mocks or a plan page with proof; options with a recommendation; the pick.
4. **Test** any new technique on a short clip; render one item per type before a batch.
5. **Build** in a copy on the latest save, layers in Premiere, one spec file driving picture, placements and sound.
   Hold heavy renders if the user is busy.
6. **Self-check** before anyone sees it: sheets, both sides of every cut, every changed time in Premiere, text and faces,
   loudness, clipped words, stray frames, facts. Fix before delivery under the same name.
7. **Deliver** new versioned files, build notes, placeholders listed, a listening note for sound, and a short report:
   which file, what changed per note, what you kept, what you could not check, at most 3 questions with defaults.
8. **Review round.** Take the notes in, scope them, build on his latest save, version, record dated rules, change the
   tool defaults, update the taste list. Then back to step 3.

## Format cheat sheet
| Format | Hook | Cut and pace | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| Long cutdown of raw footage (16:9, Facebook) | 7 to 9 s, 4 beats: premise, threat, action, twist; never the outcome | keep about 70 to 78%; cut inside arcs, never whole arcs; story in source order | over 10 min where the story allows (tentative) |
| Standalone segment | its own hook; a 3-beat premise if it starts mid-story | a subset of the long cut | 5 to 6 min |
| One-story short | open on the action; no separate hook | word-tight, no pause over 0.3 s, out 0.2 s after the last word | follows the story (0:27 to 1:32) |
| Premium talking-head ad (9:16) | key message inside 3 s, the talent's own line, never the logo | no 4 s stretch without a visual change; a change about every 3 s; shots 0.9 s or longer; hard cuts on phrase breaks | 21 to 34 s (research), plus a 5 s CTA end |
| YouTube explainer (16:9) | contrast hook in 5 s, thesis in 10 s | a change about every 3.4 s, one movement at a time | about 10 min plus a 15 to 20 s end screen |
| News reel (Reels) | headline and face from frame 0; the fact by 3 to 7 s | cut on the hook or chorus, never the intro | 14 to 36 s, loops |
| Faceless native post | a situation, shock or POV in 4 to 12 words; proposition by 3 s | one continuous shot with one line, or 3+ hard cuts per 10 s | moments 6 to 20 s, stories 25 to 60 s |
| Music edit (9:16, a client's song) | frame 1 moving on the downbeat; the song audible from frame 0 | 0.6 to 1.3 cuts a second; 20 to 35% of frames in effects; payoff zone at 55 to 70% | median 39 s, plus a 13 s loop |

## When it feels wrong
| The note | Usually means | First moves |
|---|---|---|
| "slow", "corporate" | long stretches with no visual change, no stakes | dead-stretch map; real footage of the talent; faster changes; music with stakes |
| "boring" | plain footage, no tension, a recap | something designed on plain B-roll; a hook that promises; an open loop |
| "cheap", "basic", "PowerPoint" | the treatment looks made-up or templated | real material, real recordings; optical over rigid geometry; study the top references |
| "cluttered", "too much text" | too many things at once | one thing at a time; show it instead of labelling it |
| "jumpy", "an error" | unbridged jumps, flash shots, clipped words | 1 to 3 s bridges; hard cuts on phrase breaks; remove 5-frame shots |
| "looks really bad", "not worth posting" | the concept is wrong | three new concepts with the real audio, or a research-backed plan page; do not polish |
