# Reference looks (measured, from the reference set)

**These are references, not our house style.** Our house looks (the news-page look on native pages, the premium ad
look, "premium calm" explainers, the band layout for music edits) are in captions-and-on-screen-text.md and
taste-verdicts.md and win by default. Use a look below only when a client asks for it or sends that creator as a
reference, and then copy ONE look exactly (mixing looks got storyboards rejected).

Everything here was measured in Oct 2026 from downloaded public videos: cuts checked by eye, captions by OCR at 10 fps
against word timings, music on separated stems, colours with a BT.709 decode. Fonts are the closest free matches found
by rendering candidates, not confirmed source fonts. Motion-only numbers are mirrored in motion-design.

## 1. AI-presenter news reel, "oxblood" template (9:16)
A news story told through one person or one public fight. A seated presenter delivers only the connective lines;
everything factual is primary evidence (the tweet, the letter, the game, the launch graphics). Three reels measured:
67 to 86 s, 175 to 219 wpm, voice from frame one, no pause over about 0.5 s.
- **Layouts:** a framed inset (B-roll at about 88% inside a dark red frame: 64 px from the sides, 102 px from top and
  bottom, a 952x1716 window, about 30 px corners); an exact 960/960 split with evidence on top, the presenter below and
  the caption on the seam; the presenter full screen (caption drops to chest level); full bleed for tweets on black,
  talks and gameplay. All three mix within a reel.
- **Palette:** frame radial gradient #53090F at the middle of each edge to #300406 in the corners; caption box fill
  #8C111C at 65 to 70% (reads #5F0A12 over black); display red #D0090A; text #FFF4EC (captions), #F4EBE5 (display). The
  presenter set is warm amber to sit in the same family.
- **Captions:** Lora Italic about 58 to 60 px in a square crimson box hugging the phrase (18 px side, 6 px top and bottom
  padding), dead centre (y 960) on B-roll, inset and split, 61 to 76% down on presenter shots; 3 to 4 words, never more
  than 8; hard swap, no animation; off while the voice reads on-screen text.
- **Display type:** Inter Medium white at -4% tracking plus DM Serif Display Italic #D0090A for the key word or number
  (about 1.3x larger), built word by word on the voice; used on the hook, "Meet [name]", the turn and big numbers. A
  hand-drawn arrow writes on over about 0.45 s to point at a person.
- **Presenter:** 5 to 10 appearances of 1.6 to 2.3 s, 13 to 27% of runtime (open loop, turn question, takeaway, close);
  slow push-ins of 2 to 4% (up to 6 to 11%) scale per second.
- **Evidence treatments:** a tweet read out on black (lines appear as spoken, earlier lines dim, captions off); a thread
  scroll with a white selection highlight on the sentence being read; a headline sweep (a selection bar crosses in about
  0.6 s); letters as tilted paper stacks swapped every 0.3 s; 3 to 5 screenshots at 0.3 to 0.5 s under one caption for a
  list of proof; the subject's own launch graphics as they are; a meme ending.
- **Cutting:** median shot 1.18 to 1.80 s; 73 to 76% of cuts land with a caption change; more than 90% hard cuts, no whip
  pans or light leaks; movement lives inside shots.
- **Sound:** a dry hook, the bed enters with a whoosh on the subject's introduction (6.7 to 13 s), music cut out for the
  turn line and the last line, 3 to 8 effects on structural moments only, low hits under numbers, voice about -14.5 LUFS,
  bed 17 to 22 dB under.
- **Rebuild in Premiere:** 1080x1920 at 30 fps (keep 25 fps avatar renders, add a 2 to 4% per second scale ramp on
  presenter shots); a colour matte with the radial gradient, B-roll nested at 88% with a 30 px rounded mask; splits as
  two 960 px halves; 7 to 10 presenter beats of 1.5 to 2.3 s in a 60 to 80 s reel.
- **Notes from the second reviewer when rebuilding it:** more continuity of motion at every cut; a light native glow
  only; captions on a Premiere caption track; a plain coloured background needs slow clean life (drifting light, grain)
  and should be used sparingly; vary the article highlight per beat (marker, lifted pull quote, spotlight, selection
  sweep, straight outline or underline; no tilted stickers or wobbly marks); do not lean on the rounded inset everywhere;
  articles full screen with camera moves; charts as a dark grid with glowing pastel bars and lines (amber, mint, peach),
  white serif values, pastel pills.
- **Worth borrowing:** open on a split for the first 1.5 to 3 s (proof on top, presenter below); give a quoted person a
  second caption colour and cut the music under them.

## 2. The same creator's older plain template
Podcast set (face about 23% of frame height); captions white medium sans with a soft drop shadow, no box, four words,
centred at 50%; median shot 1.43 s with a change every 0.91 s; much faster moves (pull-outs of 5 to 26% scale per second
on stills); a pad under the hook with a boom on the subject, the beat dropping on a name at 7.6 s, hits at each section
turn, bed 21.6 dB under; exported at 60 fps.

## 3. Designed explainer (9:16)
The explanation is redrawn as a motion-graphics system with its own palette, labels and type; the presenter is on screen
8 to 9% of the runtime (open split, pivot question, bridge, closing comment ask). Median shot 1.5 to 2.2 s but a caption
swap every 0.6 to 0.7 s.
- **Fonts:** headings, tags and numbers Barlow Condensed Bold/ExtraBold; labels JetBrains Mono ExtraBold/Medium; card and
  UI text Inter; captions Departure Mono; hook words and reveals Archivo (weight 900, width 125 for the hook); setup lines
  Instrument Serif Italic with the last word in the accent.
- **Palette:** paper about #EBEBE3 with a faint 44 px grid and noise, highlighter #F0DC1C, ink about #141616, black bars
  #121212; a night variant #020202 with panels #0A0A06, cream #EEEEE2, orange #FE6A2A, green about #5FD38D. One accent per
  reel.
- **Motion at 25 fps:** footage and presenter hard-cut; a dark panel wipes up covering 16%, 90%, 100% on three frames
  while carrying its content; a 2-frame dip to black before a reveal; italic setup words fade over 3 frames while rising
  about 18 px over 4; a reveal box appears 1 frame early and its letters rise one frame apart, 6 frames each; bar charts
  build the baseline over 8 frames and the bars over 9; document focus lightens the rest of the page to about 40% while a
  multiply-yellow sweep follows the voice. No bounce, overshoot or glow.
- **Captions:** Departure Mono about 34 px uppercase, white on a black pill at 65%, about 58 px tall, radius about 40% of
  the height, two-word chunks locked to word onsets, default y 1414 (74%), moved into free space per scene.
- **Sound:** a short intro cue, a sub hit and whoosh on the reveal, whooshes on chapter changes, a new music section at
  the pivot; beds 8.8 to 21.1 dB under.
- Their chapter dial and callout tags are part of their look, but the second reviewer rejected both for client work ("dont
  do pointers"). His music is mostly well-known film scores and commercial tracks, unlicensed for a business account.

## 4. Clip commentary (9:16)
About 42 s of a guest's talk wrapped in a 12 s setup and an 18 s recap. Setup: a name title over stage footage with the
host below for 1.5 s, credential cards slamming in on brand red with sub hits. Into the clip: a 0.25 s white light-leak
wipe with an impact and a whoosh, then the music stops completely. Quote card: a blurred, darkened copy of the talk fills
the frame, the talk in a 16:9 window, a pull quote above it; the guest's words in yellow #F7D00A with no pill, the host's
in white on a dark pill. Recap: a whoosh and a new beat 17 dB under the voice.

## 5. "Analog night" mini-doc (16:9)
Every frame looks like it played on an old TV and was filmed again. Four reels: 76 to 86 s, 175 to 208 wpm, 92 to 141
shots a minute, median shot 0.27 to 0.50 s.
- **Grade:** blacks crushed (the darkest 2% of pixels at 1 to 12 of 255), strong contrast, saturation held back except
  reds; corners measured at 43 to 58% of the centre's brightness (the After Effects rebuild bakes a vignette at about 45
  to 60%).
- **Texture:** bloom on every highlight and caption, red and cyan fringing growing toward the edges, fine noise, a soft
  re-encoded look; one reel 4:3 with rounded tube corners.
- **Motion:** hard cuts carry most of it; push-ins of 5 to 8% scale per second on stills and static clips.
- **Flashes:** 12 to 17 one- or two-frame white flashes per reel, black flicker frames, the odd inverted frame.
- **Devices:** a polaroid on black with the hook word blurred behind it; one word alone on black with a glow; a number
  over a news clip with the chyron left in; B&W portrait strips with red; a 3x3 news wall; HUD and code overlays; a CRT in
  a dark room playing the interview; footage inside giant letters; a narrow window on black; 3D scale comparisons;
  articles zoomed huge with a highlighter; clip walls swapping tiles every 2 frames.
- **Captions:** small words Playfair Display Italic, key words Arial Black or Inter Black at about -10% tracking, line
  height 7 to 8% of the frame, centred at 48%, a soft white bloom and a speckle texture in the letters; 1 to 3 words.
- **End card:** a hard cut into the logo at about 10x its final size, easing down over about 27 frames, shrinking slowly
  for 2 s, fading over 0.8 s, music playing through (4.2 to 8.7 s in all).
- **Sound:** 2 to 4 recognisable songs per reel switched on the line that opens each act (after a 1 to 3 s stop or a
  build); near-silence under real soundbites; few effects; music only 4 to 6 LU under the voice; cuts follow words.
- **The finish rebuilt in After Effects:** motion-design (native looks).

## 6. "Paper desk" mini-doc (16:9)
A brighter editorial look: pictures become physical objects on paper, cork, felt and grid paper; the camera moves across
them; captions stay small and quiet. Each reel gets a set tied to its topic.
- **Devices:** footage shrinking into a print on a page with a soft shadow; a karaoke page (the sentence in pale grey, the
  spoken word turning black); a film line over a VHS layer with the film's audio; cork boards with pins, red string and
  highlighted printouts; notification stacks; grid-paper polaroids; poster type knocked out of a red band; film gate
  frames with condensed red caps; clotheslines of photos with italic serif names; spinning photo rings; bar charts with
  brand icons.
- **Type:** Helvetica Neue Medium captions (about 50 to 55 px, centred at 50%) building word by word up to about 6 words,
  then clearing; key words jump to Bold at 3 to 4x or to condensed black caps on poster pages; an italic serif for names
  and chapters; two-size pairs.
- **Palette:** light page #F4F2F5, white card #FDFDFD, poster red #F2292E, sticker blue #3D29B4, felt green #376B3C.
- **Grade:** sources keep their own colour; archive goes black and white (62% of shots in one reel); pages light with a
  soft vignette and paper grain; prints with white borders and 20 to 30 px drop shadows at about 40%.
- **Motion:** slow push-ins on photos (3.5% a second), quicker pushes elsewhere (5 to 13% a second), drifting camera moves
  across desk sets with focus pulls.
- **End card:** two thin white diagonals slide in from the corners over the darkening last shot (about 10 frames), meet in
  an X and collapse into a small logo held on black for 1.5 s.

## 7. Mini-doc beats and rebuild spec (both mini-doc looks)
The eight beats and their edit treatment are in storytelling-and-structure.md. Rebuild spec: 1920x1080 at 25 fps
(1440x1080 for the 4:3 tube look); 70 to 90 s of voice (210 to 280 words at 175 to 205 wpm) from frame one; 90 to 110
shots a minute, median 0.4 s, a quarter of them 2 to 4 frames; a linear push-in of 4 to 10% a second on every still; every
spoken word printed on its first spoken frame, centred, no box; 5 to 15 text beats; hard cuts for 85 to 90%, plus per
reel 5 to 15 white flash frames, 2 to 6 zoom or whip blurs, 1 to 3 glitch slices, one 6 to 10 image flash montage and one
strobe; 2 to 4 tracks switched by act; mix -14 LUFS, peaks -1 dBTP. Checks before export: no shot over 3 s outside
soundbites and the end card; every name, number and claim has its own picture; every act starts on a new song or a clear
music change; music silent under every soundbite.

## 8. Launch films (16:9)
Three launch films measured: voice-over from frame 1 with no logo or music intro; hook, setup, turn, the product named 32
to 63% in, proof, a callback line, an end card with logo plus URL or QR. 127 to 202 words at about 190 wpm, nearly every
spoken word on screen as it is said, one emphasis device per phrase; cuts follow the voice phrases (the beat has no
measurable pull). Recipe: 29.97 fps, 50 to 70 s, one visual world per film, a mascot as the transition, voice -16 to -14
LUFS with music 10 to 12 LU under. The three films used completely different looks, so a brand's films need not share
one look. Films with no narrator run on music and sound design alone. Saturated brand colours shift in H.264: check them
in the export.

## 9. VC podcast cold opens (a16z and 23 opens across 12 shows)
One serif caption system per open (1 to 3 words with one metallic gold word every line or two), accents that last
frames, outside material on cream or white paper, 2 to 6 frame transitions between montage shots, and an ending that
cuts to black before the logo. The full measured look, the proportions (25 to 45 s of quotes, 10 to 15 s of titles) and
how a spec open was built for a client: podcast-trailers `references/cold-open-edits.md`.

## 10. Matching a client's brand
Keep the reference creator's motion and swap the palette and type for the client's. Pull them from the client's own
site CSS variables, brand guide and show art, never from guesses or another film, and save the source files in the job
folder. A parked or default-theme domain is not brand evidence. One show, one caption system, used every week, makes the
show recognisable from one frame.
