# Taste verdicts: what reviewers loved, what they rejected, and why

The calibration file. Every line is a real verdict from a review round, in the reviewer's own short words where they
were recorded (typos kept, names of clients and people swapped for [brackets]). Read the section for the format you
are about to make before you build, and check the finished piece against it before you show it.

**Who said it:** E = the editor, B = the lead reviewer, C = a client, R = a client's reviewer, F = a
second reviewer at another studio (their clients and pages: reference only, not our verdicts). Dates are 2026.

**How to use a verdict:** a rejection is about that placement unless the reviewer says "never" or "do not do X of this
type" (section 8). A verdict beats research, beats Claude's own recommendation, and beats a sibling skill's default.
When a new verdict arrives, add it here (dated, quoted, with the why) and change the tool default that caused it.

## 8. How far a verdict reaches
- A rejection is about that placement: "if u used it elsewhere and i didnt point it out there its fine there but here
  no" (E, 14 Oct). Light painting went at 6 to 7 s and stayed (cut to under half its length) at 25.7 s.
- Blanket bans are the ones said as bans: auras ("do not do auras of this type"), "PowerPoint" geometry (spins, box
  glitches, closing shutters), whips that show mirrored frames, black breath frames, colour floods (twice), gimmick
  motifs, the TikTok white box, breathing on stills, strangers' faces in a star's ad, fabricated numbers.
- Untouched parts of a hand re-cut are approvals; items a reviewer did not mention in a round are neither approved nor
  banned.
- A reversal replaces the old rule (B-roll "keep it light" became "ride off her fame"): keep the dated history, apply the
  newest.
- Research defaults are not verdicts: research said skip glitch, RGB split and flashes for a 40 to 65 premium audience,
  yet the editor loved RGB-split captions on the same ads; research said use the full vertical frame, they wanted the
  band; research said the white text box, he rejected it.

## 9. Patterns that repeat across every format
1. **Real beats designed.** Real footage of the subject, real headlines, the real app screen and 3D renders beat drawn
   stand-ins, data dashboards and stock wallpaper. Why: reviewers judge "cheap" by whether it looks made-up.
2. **Literal beats close enough.** The B-roll shows the exact noun or action in the line. Why: the reviewer checks the
   picture against the words.
3. **Readable on a phone beats pretty.** Plates, size, contrast, never over a face. Why: most views are on a phone.
4. **One thing at a time.** One movement, one number per frame, one look per video, one text treatment per page. Why:
   clutter was named in every format.
5. **The reviewer's taste beats research and Claude's pick.** Why: they know their audience; Claude's recommendations
   were overturned again and again.
6. **Good is not enough on showcase work** ("good wont cut we need GREAT"); on client work, clean and coherent wins.
7. **Tight, but never broken.** Hooks and shorts get cut hard; a clipped word, a mid-sentence cut or a 5-frame flash is
   an error, not pace.
8. **Do not overdo it.** "have personality dont overdo it tho", urgency "see where they would fit", "not a lot just a
   bit". Why: overdone reads as AI, spam or template.
9. **Honest.** No invented numbers, ratings, DMs or polls, no implied identity, stand-ins labelled. Why: one exposed fake
   costs the page or the brand its credibility, and some are legal risks.
10. **Never destroy what they made.** Disable, version, keep their hand edits.
