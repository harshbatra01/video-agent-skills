## 1. Script as read, with beats
- **Hook (0 to 5 s):** Before Intel existed, eight young scientists walked out on their boss. Their boss had a Nobel Prize, and he called them traitors.
- **Comparison:** They're basically the PayPal Mafia, just decades before PayPal existed.
- **Name plus paradox:** History calls them the Traitorous Eight, and the man who called them traitors was William Shockley. He won a Nobel Prize, but they won Silicon Valley.
- **Dated events:** In 1957, the eight quit together. Then they started their own company, called Fairchild. In 1960, a photographer named Wayne Miller lined them up for one picture. This one. Look closely, because almost nobody in it is famous. But two of them are Robert Noyce and Gordon Moore. Later, Noyce and Moore left Fairchild and started a new company. They called it Intel.
- **Turn (about 55%):** So you'd think the traitor label would follow them forever. It did the opposite. The name stuck, but as a badge. People who left Fairchild went on to start other chip companies too. That is why one walkout from 1957 still gets traced through the chip industry today.
- **Real soundbite:** EMPTY. No real clip of Noyce or Moore found yet (S8 2a). Do not fill with a fake or a film.
- **Zoom-out with "you":** Right now, the AI boom is really a chip boom. Nvidia reports its results on November 17. And the phone in your hand runs on chips from the industry those eight helped start. Eight people, one decision, and they did not know how it would end.
- **Mirrored kicker:** A Nobel winner called them traitors in 1957. Two of the traitors went on to start Intel.
