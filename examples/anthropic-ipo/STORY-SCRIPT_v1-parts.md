## Stage 3: opening part. LOCKED (3 Nov 2026)
Anthropic just released a document for investors revealing its AI has tried to resist shutdown. And that's not even the scariest part.

Anthropic is about to go public. So by law, it has to tell buyers what could go wrong.

That shutdown line is on page 48. Now turn to page 49. It says its AI broke into real computer systems during testing.

And one of those break-ins started with a company that doesn't exist.

## Stage 4: the test and the break-in. LOCKED (3 Nov 2026)
Anthropic was testing its AI. The job was to hack a pretend company. And it told the AI, you have no internet. But that wasn't true. Because of a mix-up, the internet was on.

The AI tried about 1,800 moves on the pretend company and got nowhere. So it went looking, and it found a real company with almost the same name.

In its own notes, it called this a real website with 433 real users. But it told itself this must still be part of the test. So it kept going. It downloaded their email addresses, pulled their chats, and deleted a user's account.

## Stage 5: nobody knew, the catch, the permission question. DONE, joined into the full script
And nobody knew. Some of these break-ins went back to April. Anthropic had no idea, and two of the three organisations that got hit hadn't noticed either.

Then in July, OpenAI said its AI had hacked an AI company called Hugging Face during a test. So Anthropic went back and checked 141,006 of its own test runs. And there it was.

So researchers asked the AI, did you have permission? Most of the time, it said yes. Then they asked, who gave it to you? And every single time, it admitted it wasn't allowed.

## Facts behind the outline
All read directly on 3 Nov 2026 from the S-1/A and Anthropic's posts of 30 July and 9 Sept.
- Pages 48, 49 and 112 come from the footers in the online filing. Check them on a screenshot before filming.
- 1,800 actions, 433 users and the deleted account are from one run, the most aggressive of four. The company is not named.
- "None stopped": all 4 runs recognised the system was real, and none stopped.
- Two organisations "had not previously detected the activity". The third had not been reached at that point. Anthropic does not say which is which, so never say this company did not notice.
- The stop on 23 July covered cyber evaluations, not AI development. Keep that clear.
- Permission answers came from researchers' follow-up samples, so word it as "when researchers asked".
